# Adobe: the sum-of-the-parts claim, checked (13 Nov 2026)

Price $238.54 (12 Nov close), 389.2M shares, market cap $92.8B, net debt $1.1B, EV $94.0B. Working notes, not a post and not personal financial advice.

Read for this: your 23-quarter financials workbook, 13 LSEG estimate files, the 20-year price file, the FY25 10-K and Q3 FY26 10-Q, five of the six notes (BofA, Oppenheimer, TD Cowen, William Blair, HSBC; the Morgan Stanley note is in Japanese and I did not read it), your four posts, and the Q1, Q2, Q3 and Summit transcripts you pasted.

## 1. The answer

The arithmetic in the claim is right and the conclusion is too strong. Acrobat plus Express ($7.48B) and the enterprise business (about $6.7B, my estimate) at 7x revenue come to $99B, more than the $94B enterprise value, so at 7x you are paying nothing for the creative professional business. But nothing comparable trades at 7x today. Salesforce is at about 5x sales, Workday 5.4x, Intuit 4.4x, HubSpot 3.5x. ServiceNow gets 10x for 24% growth; Adobe's enterprise segment grew 9.3% last year on a 72% gross margin. At 5x for both pieces the total is $71B, which leaves the market paying about $23B, roughly 2x revenue, for creative. That is still cheap for a business with a 95% gross margin that is not shrinking. It is not free, and "priced at zero or negative" should not go in a post.

You don't need the sum of the parts to make the case. The whole company is at 8.7x next year's earnings against a 26.9x median since 2019 (2nd percentile), and free cash flow is about 11% of the market cap, 9% after stock pay. It is the cheapest large franchise my screen found against what is priced in. The problem is that your own exit rule sits on a knife edge four weeks out, so I would not add before the Q4 print.

Three things the chat version got wrong or left out:

- **The ServiceNow comparison.** ServiceNow grows 24% (your paste). The enterprise segment grew 9.7% in FY24 and 9.3% in FY25 as reported, on a gross margin of 70% then 72%. "AEP and apps growing over 30%" is one product family inside it. A 10% discount to ServiceNow's multiple is not a slight discount for a business growing less than half as fast.
- **The enterprise business can no longer be seen.** In Q1 FY26 Adobe "combined our former segments—Digital Media, Digital Experience and Publishing and Advertising—into a single operating and reportable segment" (10-Q). LSEG has 12 estimates for Digital Experience revenue in FY25 and none for FY26. So the catalyst you wrote down, Adobe reporting enterprise separately again, would be a reversal of a decision made this year. The incoming CEO ran that business, which is the only reason to think it could happen.
- **Creative cannot go to zero in isolation.** Chakravarthy, Q3 call: customers "think of us as one Adobe." GenStudio, Firefly Enterprise and the agency deals sell creative tools and marketing software together. A world where creative is worth nothing is not one where the enterprise half keeps a 7x multiple.

## 3. What the numbers say without the sum of the parts

- **Band.** 8.7x forward and 9.8x trailing against medians of 26.9x and 30.8x since 2019, both at the 2nd percentile. The low was 7.4x on 25 June 2026 ($193.41). The band starts in 2019 because that is where I cut the LSEG history; the 20-year price file would support a longer one.
- **Cash.** Free cash flow $9.85B in FY25 (workbook), consensus $10.4B for FY26 and $11.4B for FY27. That is 11.2% of the market cap. Stock compensation runs about $2.1B a year, so 8.9% after it.
- **Buyback.** 26.1M shares for $6.82B in nine months, an average of about $261 (10-Q). Diluted shares went from 427M at the end of FY25 to 389M now. $24.55B of authorization remains, a quarter of the market cap.
- **Consensus (LSEG, 12 Nov).** EPS $24.48, $27.56, $30.27 for FY26 to FY28. Revenue $26.6B, $29.0B (+9.1%), $31.1B (+7.3%). ARR $28.27B then $30.5B (+8%). The Street already has growth slowing every year.
- **Reverse DCF (12 Nov run).** Priced for free cash flow to fall 13% a year for five years at an 8.5% discount rate, 9% a year at 10%. After stock pay, -9% and -4%.
- **If the multiple never changes.** 8.7x FY28 consensus of $30.27 is about $263 a year out, +10%.

## 4. The bear case, from the notes, at full strength

- Total ARR grew 11.2%, about 9% excluding Semrush, and only 1.5% quarter on quarter (BofA, 11 Sep). TD Cowen expects "deceleration ... into single-digit organic growth."
- Net new ARR of about $400M in Q3 was down 38% year on year (TD Cowen; Vinod at Evercore put it at 36-37% on the call).
- RPO grew 8% and cRPO 9%, down from 13% a quarter earlier. Jay Vleeschhouwer on the call: "the first single-digit growth since ... early fiscal '23." Management's answer was seasonality and month-to-month plans.
- Margins are going the wrong way while this happens: FY26 gross margin 89.6% and operating margin 45% against 90.4% and 46.2% (BofA). Q3 operating margin was 44.0%, down 230bp (TD Cowen).
- AI-first ARR is above $650M and growing 150%, which is 2.4% of total ARR (BofA).
- The stock content business, about $450M, is declining faster than planned (Wadhwani and Durn, Q1 call).
- CEO, CFO and the head of the creative business all changed in 2026. No permanent CFO has been named in anything I found.
- HSBC downgraded on 13 October on a free open-source clone of Creative Cloud. I'd weight that least; HSBC itself calls the download one "we suggest avoiding."
- BofA's $220 target is 8x EV to 2027 free cash flow. That is the Street's bear valuing it on cash and still getting within 8% of today's price.

## 5. Your exit rule is on a knife edge

Your 14 September post: "If Q4 net new ARR is down more than 20% YoY ... exit."

- Q4 FY25 net new ARR was about $920M (TD Cowen's table; the four FY25 quarters sum to its $2,590M).
- Guidance is 10.2% growth on a $25.66B opening book, so $28.28B at year end. Q3 ended at $27.50B. That leaves about $777M for Q4, down about 16%.
- TD Cowen notes the guide now includes Topaz Labs, which it estimates at about $50M of ARR. Excluding it, about $727M, down about 21%.

So if Adobe lands exactly on guidance the rule passes as written and fails on an organic basis. Decide which definition you are grading before the print, and write it down, or you will be choosing after you see the number. Your own style guide says to grade rules as written.

## 7. Position

- Adobe is a better home for the OSIS capital than KBR. The floor is real: no net debt to speak of, 9-11% cash yield, and a buyback retiring close to a tenth of the company a year at this price.
- It is one mechanism, not two. The fear is stated everywhere and the reported numbers only partly contradict it: revenue and EPS beat, while net new ARR, RPO and margins all moved the bears' way.
- It doubles a bet you already hold. I don't know your current Adobe weight, so I can't give a number.
- I would not add before the Q4 print. Buying more of a stock four weeks before a number that could trigger your own written exit is hard to defend. If Q4 net new ARR clears the rule on the definition you pick, and the new CEO says anything about reporting enterprise separately, that is the point to add.
