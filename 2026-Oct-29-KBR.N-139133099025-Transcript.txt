With that, let's move to slide 5. Today, there are four key messages from the quarter. First, strong execution drove profitable growth and reinforced confidence in the full year outlook. Second, sustainable tech continues to deliver stellar growth both year-over-year and sequentially, supported by strong bookings momentum and broad-based demand. Third, mission tech performed well through the expected NASA headwinds, strong margins and excellent cash conversion. And finally, the separation continues to advance with key leadership, governance and regulatory milestones in place ahead of the January 4 separation date.
Moving to slide 6. Sustainable tech delivered another strong quarter driven by structural demand shifts across energy security, food security and critical infrastructure. Book-to-bill was 1.3 times for the quarter, making another period of strong bookings and bringing trailing 12 months book-to-bill to 1.3 times as well. Backlog increased to another record at $5.8 billion, and that is up more than 50% from a year ago.
Excluding the Brown & Root joint venture, BRISK, year-to-date bill was 1.5 times, supporting a continued revenue growth outlook. BRISK booked softer, with deferred maintenance and shutdown in response to market conditions. We expect those deferrals to continue into Q4, with activity expected to recover in 2027 as critical maintenance needs are addressed.
The strong bookings secured over the last 12 months are now translating into accelerating growth and revenue increasing 13% sequentially as projects continue to ramp across the portfolio. This keeps us on track for a mid-teens full-year revenue growth outlook.
The Middle East remains a key growth driver, with project activity demonstrating resilience amid regional conflict. New PMC awards with ADNOC and Abu Dhabi, and an expanded role on Aramco's Marjan offshore field reinforce our position with strategic customers and our participation in critical energy investment.
In technology, SABIC in Saudi Arabia selected a purifier technology for a new 3,500 metric ton per day facility, a second large-scale Ammonia award this year following the Pampa award in Argentina. Together with Ammonia revamp wins in Europe, these awards demonstrate continued investment in food security and supply resilience.
We also secured another Bursath award this quarter for Kazakhstan's first sustainable aviation project, reflecting continued demand for technologies that advance decarbonisation. In critical infrastructure, we secured a central program management role at Diriyah Gate, one of Saudi Arabia's largest GIGA projects, and a major program engineering and integration award in Texas. Both extend our role in delivering complex critical infrastructure. Altogether, a record backlog and strong pipeline gives us increasing visibility into growth in Q4 and onwards into 2027.
Moving to slide 7. Turning to mission tech, the business continues to perform as expected. Michael has stepped seamlessly into the role, and the team delivered another solid quarter, including strong margins that Shad will discuss shortly. During the quarter, we secured important work supporting PATRIOT Air Missile and Defence and mission critical radio frequency engineering, extending long-standing customer relationships across high-priority national missions.
And recently, Firefly was selected as one of four directed energy systems for a passenger drone pilot focused system. As follows, its selection launches at Cape Canaveral and further demonstrates the relevance of a technology addressing critical and emerging customer needs. Both international businesses in the UK and Australia also perform well, with strong and healthy pipelines entering Q4 and 2027.
In the US, procurement timelines continue to extend, weighing on book-to-bill, which was 0.8 times, excluding the NASA insourcing debooking. Importantly, customers aren't walking away. Funding generally remains in place and priorities are intact, but projects are moving a little bit more slowly.
On NASA, insourcing continued during the quarter, consistent with what we discussed earlier this year. As the programme ramps down, we remove the remaining out-of-year revenue from the backlog to align the expected workload, bringing reported book-to-bill to 0.6 times. The anticipated 2026 revenue impact was already reflected in our guidance, so our full-year outlook remains unchanged.
Awards under protest remain largely unchanged during the quarter, and that includes the National Science Foundation Antarctica subcontract, which we secured through re-award following the GAO protest process. We are a customer on transition, although the award is not yet reflected in the backlog.
Finally, we ended the quarter with $9.9 billion of submitted proposals awaiting customer decisions, and together with the wins under protest, that represents $20.5 billion in the outside backlog, approximately 80% of which is new business, underscoring the depth of growth opportunities across the portfolio. Overall, the business is performing well. The pipeline remains robust, and recent wins reinforce Michigan Tech's strong competitive position as we prepare to launch Trinzic.
On to slide 8, and an update on the spin. My last slide today, I'll finish with a brief update on the separation progress. We continue to make excellent progress and have now completed many of the key milestones required to launch KBR and Trinzic as independent companies. The leadership teams are fully in, and the boards have largely been published on both sides. Shad Evans, KBR Inc - Chief Financial Officer, Executive Vice President    [4]
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Thanks, Stuart. I'll pick up on slide 10 with our consolidated third quarter results. The headline is another quarter of profitable growth with strong execution driving higher revenue, adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS.
Revenue was approximately $2.03 billion, up $97 million or 5% from the prior year. Growth in sustainable tech more than offset the expected NASA headwinds in mission tech, which I'll cover more in detail on the next slide. Adjusted EBITDA increased $28 million to $268 million, and adjusted EPS expanded 80 basis points to 13.2%. The improvement reflects strong execution of both businesses, including EBITDA from unconsolidated favorable portfolios and lower corporate costs. Adjusted EPS was $1.12, up $0.10, driven by stronger operating performance, lower below-the-line expenses and lower share count from repurchase activity over the last 12 months.
The quarter also included $15 million in interest income associated with a tax benefit that reduced our year-to-date tax rate below the guided range. We excluded that benefit from adjusted EPS, and our underlying tax rate remains in line with a guided range of 26% to 28%. Year-to-date adjusted operating cash flow was $371 million, which remains strong at 95%, reflecting improved collections during the quarter. We remain on pace to deliver our full-year cash flow outlook, cash flow expected to be ted towards the fourth quarter. I'll discuss the remaining collection timing considerations when I cover guidance. Overall, the quarter reflects strong execution and keeps us on track to deliver full-year expectations.
Returning to slide 11. Starting with sustainable tech, revenue increased $157 million, or 26%, to $762 million. The strong booking activity we've seen over the past year is now continuing to accelerate revenue growth. Adjusted EBITDA increased $19 million, or 15%, to $147 million. Quarterly adjusted EBITDA margin was 19.3%, compared with 21.2% in the prior year. Growth trailed revenue primarily due to higher contribution from an LNG joint venture in the period.
Importantly, excluding the LNG joint venture, the quarter-to-date adjusted EBITDA margin was 13.9%, up 80 basis points from the prior quarter. On a year-to-date basis, adjusted EBITDA margin, excluding the LNG joint venture, was 14.3%. That's consistent with our mid-teens full-year adjusted EBITDA margin outlook.
And MTS, approximately $1.7 billion, and that was down $60 million, or 1%, as anticipated reductions in NASA-related programs were partially offset by growth across defense and intel and international government covers. These NASA headwinds reflect the insourcing impact on the IMOC contract and the 2025 loss of IMSOC, which has since been insourced.
Importantly, adjusted EBITDA was $145 million, up $7 million, or 5% from prior year, with adjusted EBITDA margins exceeding 110 basis points to 11.5%. The improvement reflects higher unconsolidated joint venture contributions, favorable portfolio mix from the roll-off of lower-margin NASA programs, and the earlier-than-expected timing of favorable closeouts. Year-to-date adjusted EBITDA margin was 11.4%, which remains above the full-year margin outlook due to the timing of favorable closeouts. Margins in Q4 are expected to return to normative levels. Overall, sustainable tech delivered strong revenue and adjusted EBITDA growth, while mission tech increased profitability despite lower volume.
Turning to capital allocation on slide 12. Our balance sheet remains strong, and our approach to capital allocation remains disciplined. Net leverage ended the quarter at 2.1 times, below our 2.5 times target, providing financial flexibility as we prepare for separation. Our immediate cash focus is collecting outstanding receivables and carrying more collections momentum into year-end. During the quarter, we returned $21 million to shareholders through our regular dividend. As we approach separation, we're taking a prudent approach to capital allocation, funding the remaining one-time spin-related costs while preserving flexibility and strong balance sheets for companies.
Turning to slide 13. Based on our year-to-date performance and current visibility, we are reaffirming up-to-year 2026 guidance on all metrics. Outlook assumes flat to modestly lower sequential revenue in mission tech and continued low double digits in sustainable tech, supported by strong bookings and demand trends we continue to see across the portfolio.
Cash collection timing remains an area of focus, particularly in the Middle East. While the conflict has not changed our outlook, continued delays in customer collections could shift approximately $25 million to $50 million of cash flow beyond year-end. Despite that potential timing, we expect to remain in our full-year cash flow guidance range.
With that, I'll hand it back to Stuart.
