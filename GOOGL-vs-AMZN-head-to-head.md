The bake-off for the "cleanest AI-ROI, lowest-idiosyncratic-risk, most-derated beneficiary" slot.
Both analyzed on the same framework (4 calls, TIKR estimates to 2030, Q3'26 10-Qs, sell-side).
Prices: **GOOGL $265.29 · AMZN $246.82** (Nov 20 close). This is analysis, not advice.

> **Headline finding that changes the GOOGL thesis:** own-silicon is **NOT** a unique GOOGL edge.
> Amazon's **Trainium** is at least as strong as Google's **TPU** — it has **both** Anthropic *and*
> OpenAI as multi-gigawatt anchors, **$225B+ of revenue commitments**, Graviton (the leading CPU
> chip) alongside it, and Amazon will sell Trainium externally from late 2027. So "free TPU
> optionality" is a hyperscaler story, not a Google differentiator. Correct the GOOGL docs accordingly.

## Where AMZN is genuinely BETTER
1. **AWS is the better cloud business on the numbers.** Bigger ($184B run-rate vs ~$123B), higher
   margin (38.7% vs 36.2%), and **accelerating six straight quarters** (+24%→+28%→+37%→+39%) — vs
   Google Cloud's lumpier +103% (flattered by low-margin TPU *hardware* gross-up). Crucially, Amazon
   **guided AWS >40% in BOTH 2027 and 2028** and says it's "sold out through a good chunk of 2028."
   That's more forward conviction than Google's "significant increase." Backlog $578B (+189% YoY).
2. **FCF inflects a year earlier** — consensus +$5.5B in 2028 vs GOOGL's 2029 — and Amazon has the
   longer, proven track record of capex→ROIC conversion ("we've done this before in the first era of
   cloud").
3. **The retail engine is a real, separate compounder** — NA/Intl margins expanded ~10pts in a few
   years, units +15% YTD, grocery/perishables inflecting, and **Advertising is an ~$83B high-margin
   business growing ~20%** (Amazon's quiet profit engine, the YouTube-subs analog). Op income +61% YoY,
   13.9% consolidated margin (record).
4. **Slightly cheaper on EV/EBITDA** (9.9x vs 10.3x FY27) and the Street is uniformly bullish.

## Where GOOGL is genuinely BETTER
1. **Cheaper and far more derated** — 17.8x vs 23.0x FY27 P/E, and **−35% off its high vs AMZN's −14%.**
   GOOGL offers much more margin of safety; its price is more disconnected from its fundamentals.
2. **Fortress balance sheet** — **net cash ~$115B vs AMZN's net debt ~$191B.** In a VIX-36 tape with
   >$1T of capex commitments industry-wide, that matters.
3. **Lower beta (1.23 vs 1.50)** — less drawdown in a flush; the better "ballast."
4. **The risk is a single, trackable binary** (Search commercial-query monetization) you can monitor
   quarter-to-quarter and in alt-data. AMZN's risks are more diffuse — valuation leaves less cushion,
   retail carries **consumer-spend cyclicality** (a recession hits AMZN's stores in a way it doesn't hit
   Search ads as hard), net debt, and the largest absolute capex in the group.
5. **GAAP-to-clean is the same caveat on both** (equity marks inflate FY26 EPS), so neither wins there.

## Where they're a WASH

- **Cloud backlog** ($561B vs $578B — both floored).
- **EV/EBITDA** (~10x both).
- **The FCF desert** (both burning FCF for 2–3 years on AI capex; both inflect late-decade).
- **Equity-mark EPS distortion** (both; use FY27 clean).

## The verdict for your slot

**It's close, and the honest answer is they're the two best-positioned names in the complex — and the
cleanest expression may be owning BOTH as the hyperscaler core, because each hedges the other's main
risk:** if Search commercial monetization disappoints, AWS doesn't care; if consumer/retail softens,
Search/Cloud is insulated. They share the own-silicon upside and the backlog-floored cloud demand.

**If you must pick ONE for the exact criteria you've been using** — *value + balance sheet + most-
derated-vs-fundamentals + a single trackable risk* — **GOOGL still edges it:** it's ~5 turns cheaper on
P/E, twice as derated (−35% vs −14%), net cash vs net debt, lower beta, and its one risk is monitorable.
You're paying up for AMZN's (better) momentum right at a −14% dip, with net debt and a higher beta.

**But if the criterion is "best AI business / best cloud,"** AWS wins — bigger, higher-margin,
accelerating, >40% guided both years, same silicon story. Needham/JPM's "$1T revenue streams justify
the capex / almost can't help but buy" is a fair read of the *business*; the question is the *entry*.

**Practical shape:** make **GOOGL the larger core starter here** (more margin of safety at the 52-wk
low), and **add AMZN on weakness** — it hasn't derated much, so wait for a flush toward the low-$200s /
~20x FY27 before sizing up, or start smaller. Together they're a cleaner "own the AI-ROI capturers +
the alternative silicon" core than either alone, and a better barbell ballast against the NVDA torque
stub than picking just one.

## The one correction this forces on the GOOGL thesis
The GOOGL docs lean on "TPU / own-silicon optionality is nearly free and a structural NVDA short." That
leg is **real but not differentiating** — AMZN has the same (arguably bigger) merchant-silicon story.
So the GOOGL *edge* narrows to **valuation + balance sheet + the single trackable risk**, not "unique
silicon." The NVDA-short logic still holds for the barbell — it's just that **both** GOOGL and AMZN are
the listed expressions of "un-scarce the shovel," not GOOGL alone.
