## 1. The answer

The market was right to sell the investor day, and I was wrong about why. I told you the 20% segment margin "was not the business" as if that were yesterday's news. It wasn't. Management has broken out the LNG joint venture every quarter since 5 May 2026, and both notes model it. What was new on 11 November was the layer underneath: the standalone company starts at about 10% margin, not 14%, once corporate cost is allocated and the UK pension goes to Trinzic, and the growth floor is 10% where the 2024 target was 11-15%. That took the best-case 2027 EBITDA for the remaining company from about $480M (Truist's $535M less the new corporate cost) to "in excess of $425M". On my corrected sum of the parts KBR is worth about $37.6, 4% above price. That puts it behind OSIS, Napco and Trimble, and I would not buy it before the spin. The reason to keep watching is the 19.9% Trinzic stake KBR has to sell within about twelve months, on top of holders who don't want a shrinking NASA contractor. If either half is going to be mispriced, it is more likely in 2027 than now.

## 3. Was it new? What the Street already had

- **The LNG split was known.** Evans, Q1 call, 5 May 2026: services growth has left margins at "approximately 15% with the LNG project adding an incremental 500 basis points." Q2: 13% excluding LNG. Q3: 13.9%. Wells Fargo's rating rests on "visibility gaps around LNG bridge." Truist models equity income falling from $211M to $85M in 2027.
- **Consensus was already flat to down.** LSEG, 12 Nov, pre-dating any post-event revisions: EPS $4.06 (2026), $4.00 (2027), $3.96 (2028). EBITDA $1,009M, $991M, $973M. Wells had 2027 EPS at $3.83 and Truist $3.85.
- **What moved.** Truist had the remaining company at $535M of 2027 segment EBITDA (17.5% margin). Less $55M of corporate cost that is $480M. The company said above $425M. That is an 11% shortfall against the more cautious of the two segment models; D.A. Davidson was at $600M (LSEG).
- **Sizing it.** $55M at the 7.5x Wells uses is about $410M. The stock lost about $330M. On the whole company it is smaller: $425M plus a Trinzic near $490M (Truist's $552M less a similar corporate load, my assumption) is about $915M against consensus of $991M, an 8% cut for a 6.7% fall.

So the fall matches the cut. It was a repricing of a number, not a change of mind about the company.

## 5. Valuation, corrected

Wells Fargo bases its $40 target on 7.5x 2027 EBITDA, "broadly in line with its historical average." My band said 9.0x earnings is the 5th percentile since 2020. Both are true: the P/E fell because earnings quality did, while EV/EBITDA sits near 7x, close to normal. I should have shown both. On EV/EBITDA this stock is not at a trough.
