# WGC — Gold Miners Remain Resilient Despite Tighter Margins in Q2'26

Source: World Gold Council / Metals Focus. Author: Oliver Blagden (Mine Supply Analyst, Metals Focus). Published 10 November 2026.
URL: https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/11/gold-miners-remain-resilient-despite-tighter-margins-q226

## The "why the streamer, not the miner" data

- **Global average AISC (All-In Sustaining Costs) rose +8% q/q and +22% y/y to a record US$1,930/oz in Q2'26.** 29th consecutive y/y increase.
- Drivers: higher sustaining capex, operating expenses, production disruption; partly offset by lower royalties.
- **Sustaining capex +34% q/q and +25% y/y to US$441/oz (record)** = ~80% of the US$139/oz q/q net AISC rise. Sustaining capital's share of cost base up to 23% from 18% in Q1'26.
- **Cash Operating Costs +6% q/q, +18% y/y to US$1,215/oz.** Total Cash Costs +4% q/q, +22% y/y to US$1,406/oz. Labour and diesel key pressures.
- Royalty/production-tax costs −11% q/q to US$185/oz (but +53% y/y).

## Single-mine blowups (the idiosyncratic risk streamers avoid directly)

- **B2Gold Goose mine (Canada): AISC US$6,390/oz** — catch-up deferred capex + a fire in the crushing circuit restricted throughput.
- Newmont Cadia (Australia): extra costs during downtime after an April seismic event; returned to normal mid-June.

## Margins

- Average gold price −7.2% q/q to US$4,512/oz. **Average AISC margins contracted −16% q/q to US$2,582/oz** (≈2.2× the price move — downside operating leverage), reversing part of Q1's expansion. Still +52% y/y and the second highest in the dataset.
- Cash generation stayed strong; balance sheets robust (Newmont, Agnico Eagle, Kinross ended June with net cash of US$3.4bn, US$3.3bn, US$1.9bn).

## Outlook

- AISC approaching US$2,000/oz; expensive fuel purchases and supply-chain/inflationary pressure expected to sustain cost increases. Q3'26 gold averaged US$4,266/oz (−5.5% q/q), ~US$4,040/oz at time of writing → "sets the stage for further margin contraction over the remainder of 2026."

Contrast for the thesis: **WPM's cost is contractually fixed (~20% of spot / ~US$600/oz)**, so none of this AISC inflation, fuel exposure, or single-mine blowup risk hits its margin directly. That asymmetry is the core "why the streamer" case.
