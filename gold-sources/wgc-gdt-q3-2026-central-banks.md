# WGC Gold Demand Trends Q3 2026 — Central Banks

Source: World Gold Council, Gold Demand Trends: Q3 2026, "Central Banks" section. Published 3 November 2026.
URL: https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-demand-trends/gold-demand-trends-q3-2026/central-banks
Reported data captured to 30 October 2026; delayed reporting could result in subsequent revisions.

## Headline

- Central bank demand reached **321t in Q3 2026**, well above the five-year quarterly average (227t) and above the solid Q2 total (296t).
- **Y-t-d purchases total 675t, 6% above the same period last year.**
- Reported Q3 buying of **120t** remained highly concentrated, led by China and Poland.

| Tonnes | Q3'25 | Q3'26 | y/y % change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central banks and other institutions | 226.3 | 321.2 | +42% |

## Reported vs estimated (the gap)

- Reported net buying = **120t** in Q3 vs total estimated demand of **321t**.
- **Unreported activity accounted for ~63% of the quarter's demand**, continuing the pattern since 2022. Disclosed country-level purchases give only a partial picture.

## Who bought

- **China and Poland together added 104t (~86% of reported net purchases).**
  - PBoC largest buyer: **+63t** (China's Q3 accelerated, >half its y-t-d buying).
  - National Bank of Poland: **+40t** (slowed after a strong Q2 of 51t).
- Kazakhstan +14t, Uzbekistan +14t (only others reporting >5t).
- Czech Republic +5t (= 6% of holdings at quarter start — rapid pace for a smaller buyer). Multi-year accumulation continues for China, Poland, Czech Republic despite elevated prices.
- Several buyers (Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Ghana, Bolivia) are significant gold producers → role of domestic purchase programmes.

## Sales

- Generally limited. Small cuts by Qatar, Kyrgyz Republic, UAE.
- **Russia −19t**, the only sizeable reduction reported during the quarter.

## WGC framing

The stronger Q2/Q3 pace coincided with an easing of the average price from its Q1 high and lower volatility. "Although this moderation may have supported purchases at the margin, prices remained historically elevated, suggesting that longer-term strategic considerations, such as diversification and risk management, have remained the primary driver of demand."
