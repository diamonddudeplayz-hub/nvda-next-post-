# AI Bubble / Macro Thesis — Handover

Date: Nov 1, 2026. Context: worked through the whole "is this an AI bubble, and what do I do about NVDA" question over one long session, triggered by OpenAI paring its raise ($30B target → ~$11B committed at $1.25T) and a brutal week (Nasdaq -2.9% Fri, Intel -28%, Meta credit outlook cut, hyperscaler capex light, Alphabet worst-ever day). This captures the thesis, the decision, the dashboard, and the post angles.

---

## The one-line view

It's a real technology with bubble financing. Both are true at once, which is the historical norm (the internet was real and 1999 still happened). The tech arrives; the overexcited capital gets a haircut. The question was never "is it a bubble" — it's **which names survive the right-sizing**, and I own/want the survivors, not the overbuilders.

The cleanest formulation I landed on: **"It will come. I just don't know if it comes at the rate they need it to."** The spending pace has run ahead of the demand pace, and the gap between those two rates is where the pain lives. Too fast, too soon.

---

## The argument, built from the ground up

### 1. The 9%-of-GDP anchor (the staggering, falsifiable one)
Columbia's Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh (Brookings paper): to justify the current build-out at a 10% unlevered ROIC, AI revenue has to hit ~$3.5 trillion by 2032 — **8.8% of GDP.** That's as big a line as food, ~2x all energy, ~7x phone+streaming+internet combined. Software+IT today is only 3-4% of GDP. The whole number rests on one fragile assumption: that AI keeps charging today's **scarcity prices** in 2032, when capacity has quadrupled and competition is everywhere. (This is the macro version of my own GPU-rent point — scarcity pricing props up the economics now and unwinds when the shortage ends.)

Honest calibration: 9% is probably too high on that timeline at this pace, but it's not 9%-or-zero. Demand probably grows to *something less* — maybe 4-5% — which justifies *some* of the build but not all. The reckoning is a **right-size to real demand, and the gap between the build and the real demand is the loss that gets written down.** Depth depends on where real demand lands (5% = big write-down, survivable; 2% = catastrophic). Unknowable variable.

### 2. Jevons vs the computer template (the unresolved crux)
- **Jevons (bull):** cheaper + better spurs more than enough demand to offset falling prices. Effective price per unit of capability falling ~47%/quarter (Epoch AI), 6x faster than compute, and demand is *still* outrunning it. Google token volume: ~480T/mo (May '25) → ~980T (Sep) → ~1,300T (Oct) → reportedly 3.2 quadrillion/mo, ~7x YoY. Jevons is visibly still running.
- **Computer template (bear):** PCs got cheaper forever but demand plateaued once ubiquitous and the killer apps were built (VisiCalc revolutionized, Excel upgrades couldn't). AI could saturate the same way.
- **The tell to watch:** not the token *level* but the *growth rate*. Tunguz flagged Google's monthly token growth already slowed (+250T → +107T, May→Jul). Jevons ending looks like the growth rate rolling over while capex is still priced for it continuing. Second derivative.
- Is AI a computer (saturates) or electricity (a general-purpose input that expands for decades)? That's the entire bull/bear axis. Unknown. But note: even electricity took ~40 yrs to pay off, computers ~25 (Solow's "computers everywhere except the productivity stats"). The Harvard/Jellyfish study is that lag live: AI boosted code output 12-30% but completed-project gains were statistically insignificant because humans spent the saved time reviewing. **Productivity can rise without revenue rising proportionally** — that's the bridge where the bear case lives.

### 3. Human nature is the root (the deepest level)
Why the spend *always* runs ahead of demand, and why it recurs:
- **Capital front-runs the lag.** The tech-to-payoff lag is physics (40 yrs electricity, 25 computers). Capital is impatient and doesn't wait. There's *always* a window where the money's been spent and the payoff hasn't arrived — that window is the bubble. Recurs every time because the lag is physics and the impatience is psychology; neither changes.
- **Competitive FOMO forces the overspend (this is what makes it *too fast*).** No hyperscaler CEO can under-build if AI is the next platform: downside of overbuilding = "wasted money," downside of underbuilding = "lost the franchise, fired." The asymmetry is the engine. Rational individuals → collectively irrational overbuild. An arms race where nobody can blink.
- **Knowing the history doesn't immunize you.** Everyone read about 1999. Doesn't matter, because (a) "this time is different" is always *partly true* (the tech is real, like it was in '99), and (b) the incentives operate in the present — the CEO who sits out gets fired before the bust vindicates him. Chuck Prince, 2007: "as long as the music is playing, you've got to get up and dance." He *knew*. He danced anyway. The bubble isn't stupidity; it's smart people structurally forced to participate. That's why it'll happen again after AI too.
- **The other half of the coin (the opportunity):** the same psychology that overshoots *up* (FOMO) overshoots *down* (panic liquidates the good with the bad). The discount-to-intrinsic-value on the *survivors* in the down-overshoot is the gift. "Be greedy when others are fearful" is a statement about the down-overshoot. Up-overshoot = the risk; down-overshoot = the opportunity; same human nature, opposite ends.

### 4. The arms race is now de-escalating (the live inflection)
"Nobody can blink" held only while the market *rewarded* spending. The scoreboard just flipped: MSFT trimmed capex ($175B → $170B), Tesla cut, Alphabet keeping it tight, and **Wall Street is rewarding the discipline** while punishing over-spenders (Meta's credit outlook cut, Alphabet's drop on an AI-monetization miss). When the market pays for restraint, they *can* blink, and they are. **Key consequence: the capex discipline that saves the hyperscalers' stocks is the demand cut that hits NVDA.** A trimmed capex dollar was a chip. So the pullback is bullish for the disciplined mega-caps and bearish for the picks-and-shovels supplier. The plateau isn't theoretical — it's in the capex guides now.

## What happens to the spenders (the sorting, not a uniform collapse)
- **Hyperscalers (MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN/META):** eat it but survive. Write-downs, poor ROIC on the capex, a de-rating, a quiet capex slowdown (= the GDP air-pocket), overbuild becomes "capacity we grow into at worse ROIC." Punishment to the stock and returns, not to the existence. They have ads/cloud/retail/Office to absorb it.
- **Neoclouds (CoreWeave et al.):** die. Borrowed to build, no other business. Can't service/refi the debt → bankruptcy (Musk & Gartner: most neoclouds fail). Assets (powered buildings, GPUs) sell at distressed prices to survivors. The fiber-1999 outcome exactly.
- **Labs:** sort by unit economics. The burners with no path (OpenAI if funding keeps tightening) rationalize hard or break; the ones near paying their own way (Anthropic) survive, pace slows.
- **NVDA (supplier):** demand hit (ACIE) + Lucent hit (write-downs on its $99B ecosystem stakes, guarantee calls, 10% CoreWeave) → revenue decelerates (Cisco). Survives on cash flow + moat, but de-rates and disappoints.

The bifurcation, pointed at the spenders: cash-flow players survive with poor returns; debt-funded players get culled and their assets transfer; the supplier gets hit through the chain.

## NVDA specifically

- **14x forward is "de-rated," not "cheap."** Cheap is conditional on the E holding. If guidance rolls over (Cisco-style), it's 14x on a shrinking number and it de-rates further. Don't call it cheap flatly.
- **It's directly entangled, not a clean downstream play.** Via the backing — the $99B stakes, ~$108B guarantees, 10% of CoreWeave, $500B announced financing platforms — if the neoclouds/OpenAI crack, NVDA takes the Lucent hit (write-down + guarantee call + order collapse in the same quarter). My own Post 2 had both legs: hit *last* near-term (already paid for shipped chips, guarantees not live yet), but the *Lucent leg* activates in a real crack.
- **Sizing of the direct hit:** ~$164B worst case ≈ 37% of FY28 EBITDA, ~3% of market cap. Real but survivable. The *bigger* hit is the order book (ACIE), not the write-downs.
- **The Cisco anchor:** real winner, survived, but customer capex reversed → revenue fell ~15%, $2.2B inventory writeoff, stock -90%, 20+ yrs to recover *because it was 130x.* NVDA at 14x avoids the 20-yr coma (no 130x of air) but NOT the earnings-decline drawdown if the buildout reverses. 14x shields the *valuation* reset, not the *earnings* fall.
- **The "committed spend" timing nuance:** near-term orders are propped by money already committed, so the crack doesn't come *now* — it comes at the **renewal** of the capex wave (2-4 quarters out). Watch new bookings/commitments vs. just backlog delivering. New commitment growth rolling over while backlog ships = the cliff approaching.

## The scenario spectrum (rough weights, honest lean)

1. **Soft landing / dip was the gift (~25-30%).** Nov prints hold (OpenAI closes $20B, CoreWeave refis, ACIE grows, Fed pause). Correction, not bust. Shorts squeeze, relief rally. Live — don't dismiss.
2. **Rolling repricing / slow grind (~35-40%, modal).** Committed capex carries near-term, then renewal slows, rates stay high, weak neoclouds squeezed, complex de-rates gradually over 6-18 mo, choppy. Air leaks out slowly. NVDA de-rates but survives.
3. **Real bust / cascade (~20-25%, risen this month).** Trigger needed. **Chokepoint = first neocloud refi failure** → sector credit freeze → GPU glut → rent collapse → NVDA Lucent hit → hyperscaler capex cuts → Mag 7 re-rate (~35% of S&P) → index -20-30% → wealth effect → recession → Fed cut → floor.
4. **Melt-up then bust (~10-15%).** Relief rally first (new highs into '27), *then* the 9%-too-much reality busts it from higher. The "Burry was early" path. Punishes selling early.

**Lean:** slow grind modal; bust tail has grown; bounce is live and decided fast. Right now (this week): leaning cautious-bear but **unresolved**, forked by the November prints.

## The tail that breaks the whole thesis: AI gets smarter than humans

Everything above is a bet on **pace** — does ordinary adoption show up fast enough to justify the spend. The smarter-than-human case is a different axis entirely: a bet on a **capability discontinuity**, not an adoption curve. If it lands, the frame inverts and the bear case is simply wrong.

- **The overbuild becomes an underbuild.** If models can do real knowledge work (replace a worker, run R&D, operate autonomously), the demand ceiling isn't 9% of GDP — it's a claim on labor income (~55-60% of GDP). Against that, $3.5T of capex is *small*. Scarcity pricing holds and extends. The computer-template (saturation) bear case dies; it's Jevons all the way up. NVDA isn't overbuilt, it's short.

Why it's still not the whole trade (the honest catches):

1. **Right about the revolution ≠ right about the position.** 1999 bulls were directionally correct about the internet and still lost ~85%, because they paid for 2010's demand in 1999 and the cash-flow wall came first. "AGI is coming" and "NVDA at $204 is a buy" are different statements. You can win the thesis and lose the trade on the path.
2. **The revolution cuts the bull case too.** Mass labor displacement (the "if everyone gets cut off" point) introduces unpriceable political/regulatory risk *even if the tech wins*: windfall taxes, nationalization pressure, House-resolution politics. The technology capturing the economy doesn't mean *shareholders* capture the gains.
3. **Double-edged for NVDA even on the upside.** Near-term a monster tailwind. But the moat is "we're the scarce shovel." A superintelligence that designs better chips — or makes Trainium/TPU good enough — *un-scarces the shovel*. The thing that proves the demand also threatens the pricing power.

**What it means for the decision:** this tail is the single best argument *against* trimming to zero — and it is NOT an argument against cutting a 36% concentration in a ~$10k account. They coexist. Keep a stake so if the revolution rips you still participate; cut the concentration so being wrong on timing (Scenario 3) doesn't break you. **Selling 10 of 17 and holding 7 *is* that barbell** — sized to survive the bust and still win the melt-up. The right shape precisely *because* this can't be ruled out. Don't let the AGI tail talk you out of de-risking; let it stop you from going flat.

## Where it's priced / NOT priced (I over-claimed "consensus," corrected)
- **Already repriced:** NVDA (30x → 14x), neoclouds (CoreWeave -50%, 16% bonds, record CDS), Intel -28%.
- **NOT priced:** a real earnings reversal (14x prices a slowdown, not a decline), and the **broad S&P still near highs (only ~7-8% off)** — the systemic/GDP blast-radius scenario is not in the index. The un-priced risk lives in the broad index and the expensive mega-caps, NOT in already-de-rated NVDA.
- **Sampling caveat:** "it's consensus" was oversampled from the bear echo chamber (r/AIBubble, QTR). r/NvidiaStock still bullish; price/positioning still bullish (index near highs). It's a *turning narrative*, not settled consensus. Retail already scared = usually a late signal, nearer bottoms than tops. So "more downside" is not a lock, and the bear trade is crowded in commentary even if not in price.
- **Absolute-dollar point:** Mag 7 ≈ 35% of the S&P. A 15-20% drop in a $3-4T name = $500-800B vaporized *per name*, regardless of the modest % — hits 401ks, pensions, wealth effect, consumption. Blast radius in dollars is unprecedented because the caps are.

## The master macro variable: the long-end yield
Fiscal dominance (big-beautiful-bill deficits, heavy Treasury issuance, thinning foreign CB buyer) keeps risk-free yields elevated. Yields = the discount rate for everything → (a) compress long-duration AI valuations, (b) make the debt-funded build harder to refinance. Near-term prints can be fine while the medium-term refi wall steepens. **This is the same thesis as my gold book** (fiscal dominance, China dumping Treasuries, Treasury doubling buybacks). Gold is the *hedge* for the exact regime that threatens the AI build. One number ties both books: **the 30Y yield.** It climbs → caution + gold both right. It rolls over → AI relief + gold's catalyst softens.
Note: the rate/refi squeeze is *concentrated* in the below-IG, debt-funded parts (neoclouds). NVDA is net cash (no refi need) — hit *indirectly* via customers. IG hyperscalers refi fine. Epicenter again = the stuff I don't own.

## The decision (made this weekend)
- **Context:** small university account, ~$10k. NVDA was ~36% ($3,644, ~17 shares) — a *big single-name concentration*, not a small position (I initially mis-sized it as small — corrected).
- **Action:** sell ~10 of 17 NVDA shares, down to a weight I'd hold calmly through a 40% drop (~15-20% of book). Rotate proceeds into **GLD** (not cash).
- **Why it's right, on two independent grounds:**
  1. *Sizing* — 36% in one volatile name in a $10k account is too much, true regardless of any macro call. This alone justifies it.
  2. *Thesis* — NVDA is the supplier exposed to the capex plateau that's now visibly beginning; the discipline rewarding the hyperscalers is the demand cut hitting NVDA. And GLD hedges the fiscal/rate macro that pressures the whole complex.
- **It's a thesis-and-sizing trim, not a price-timing bet** — so it survives being wrong on timing. GLD caveat: it's swapping one conviction (NVDA) for another (gold, mid-correction ~$3,900) that happens to be uncorrelated — a rotation between convictions, not a flight to safety. Fine because I hold the gold thesis.
- **The early risk:** prices haven't moved much (S&P near highs), so I may watch NVDA bounce first (Scenario 1/4). Early like Burry. But early-and-small survives the wait; late-and-big doesn't. Right error for the account.
- **Have the buy-back list + levels ready** for the down-overshoot in the *survivors* (quality hyperscalers, NVDA at a deep discount with moat intact). The game: size down into the froth, buy the survivors into the panic.
