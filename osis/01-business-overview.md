# OSI Systems (OSIS) — What The Hell Do They Actually Do

*Plain-English business overview. Sources: FY2025 10-K (Item 1, Business), Q1 FY27 10-Q,
Aug 2026 Q4 call, BofA fireside (May 2026). As of 9 Nov 2026.*

---

## One sentence
OSI makes the **X-ray and scanning machines that governments use to look inside cargo, bags,
vehicles and people** — and it's the **#1 player in the world in CARGO/VEHICLE/BORDER inspection
(>50% share)**, though only **#2–3 in airport passenger/baggage screening (~20–25%, behind Smiths
Detection)**. See `07-FAQ-and-deep-answers.md` §1. Bolted onto that are a
component-manufacturing arm that supplies the guts for those machines (and sells the same parts
to Fortune 500 OEMs), and a small hospital patient-monitoring business.

Think of it as: **"the TSA/customs scanner company"** — but the real story is that it's quietly
become a **recurring-revenue + defense-radar** business wearing a lumpy-hardware costume.

## The three divisions

### 1. Security (~70% of revenue) — brand name: **Rapiscan / AS&E / S2**
This is the whole thesis. They design and sell **non-intrusive inspection (NII)** systems — the
big X-ray portals that scan:
- **Cargo & vehicles** at ports, borders, checkpoints (trucks, shipping containers, rail cars).
  *This is where OSI is the global #1.*
- **Checked & carry-on baggage** at airports (aviation screening).
- **People** (checkpoint body scanners), plus trace detection (explosives/narcotics sniffers)
  and radiation/nuclear-material monitors.
- **Turnkey "Security-as-a-Service"**: OSI will build, staff and *operate* an entire screening
  checkpoint for a government and bill it over years (the Mexico program was this model).

**Customers:** US Department of Homeland Security (CBP/TSA), DoD, State, plus the governments of
~150 countries. High-profile venues too — UK Parliament, the Vatican, the Olympics, FIFA World
Cup, Super Bowl. They're the screening provider for **LA28 Olympics**.

**Newer, important piece — RF / defense (inside Security):** OSI bought an RF (radio-frequency)
business ~18 months ago. It makes **over-the-horizon radar** transmit subsystems. This is now
plugged into **Golden Dome** (the US integrated missile-defense initiative) and the **SHIELD
IDIQ**. It won a single **~$235M** over-the-horizon radar award — their largest RF deal ever —
plus ~$90M more in bookings. Management calls the acquisition "a grand slam."

**Why it's defensible / AI-proof:** You cannot software-away the physical screening of a
shipping container or a passenger. It's **mandated by regulation** (TSA, CBP, EU 100%-cargo-scan
mandates), mission-critical, and approval/certification-gated (regulators must certify each
system). More trade security, more border scrutiny, more defense spend = more OSI.

### 2. Optoelectronics & Manufacturing — "Opto" (~20% of revenue) — brand: **OSI Optoelectronics**
Makes **optoelectronic devices** (sensors, detectors, photodiodes — things that turn light into
electrical signals) and provides **contract electronics manufacturing** (circuit boards, cable/
harness assemblies, flex circuits). Sells to Fortune 500 OEMs in **aerospace & defense, medical,
industrial**.

The clever part — **vertical integration**: Opto supplies the key components that go *into* OSI's
own Security and Healthcare products. Those intercompany sales get eliminated in consolidation,
but **the margin stays in-house**. OSI literally started as an optoelectronics company and grew
Security and Healthcare on top. This is their structural edge: they control their own supply chain
and capture more of the margin stack. Opto also benefits from the **"diversify away from China"**
supply-chain shift (they manufacture in US, UK, Canada, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Mexico).

### 3. Healthcare (<10% of revenue) — brand: **Spacelabs**
Hospital **patient monitoring** (bedside monitors, telemetry) and **cardiology** (ECG/Holter,
ambulatory blood pressure). Sells to hospitals in the US, UK, Germany, Saudi, Asia. Recurring
revenue through service, spare parts, consumables.

**The problem child.** Smallest division, has been shrinking (hospitals deferring capital
spend — Q1 FY27 revenue was **down 14% YoY** and the division ran a small operating *loss*). BUT
it carries the **highest contribution margins** at OSI, so even a modest revenue recovery flips it
to meaningful profit. New management team installed, next-gen monitoring platform coming (revenue
impact more FY28). Optionality, not a current driver.

## How they make money (the mechanics that matter)

1. **Sell the box (product revenue).** Lumpy, project-driven, big international and government
   orders. Comes with a ~1-year warranty.
2. **Then sell the service (recurring revenue).** As that installed base rolls **off warranty**,
   it converts to high-margin service/maintenance/SaaS contracts. Service carries **>10 points
   higher gross margin** than product. This is the margin-expansion flywheel.
3. **Recurring mix today ≈ 30% of Security** (≈25% of total company), steering to **40%+** within
   Security. ⚠️ **Reality check:** this lifts blended margin only *slowly* — service is ~25% of total
   and creeps up ~1pt/yr, so segment mix (more Opto, Mexico) has actually kept **gross margin FLAT-
   to-DOWN** (36% in 2017→33% FY26). Analysts model margins ~flat, not expanding. EPS growth comes
   from **revenue + buybacks**, not a margin explosion. See `07-FAQ.md` §4. Don't over-weight this.
4. **SaaS layer:** CertScan and similar platforms let customs/border agencies review scans
   remotely over secure networks — a genuine software annuity sitting on top of the hardware.

## The moat, in one list
- **#1 global share** in cargo/vehicle inspection (scale + reference wins compound: win one big
  sovereign contract, the next country takes note).
- **Regulatory certification** gating — systems must be approved by TSA/EU/etc.; not easy to enter.
- **Vertical integration** (Opto) — owns its component supply, captures margin, controls lead times.
- **Installed base → recurring service annuity** with switching costs (you service the fleet you sold).
- **Sole-source / preferred-supplier** status on key US programs (both recent CBP IDIQs: OSI is the
  *only* awardee).

## The risks, in one list
- **Lumpy, government-timing-driven revenue** — shutdowns, geopolitics and procurement delays move
  quarters around (this is exactly what caused the sell-offs; see `03-thesis-and-catalysts.md`).
- **Customer/geographic concentration** — large single contracts (Mexico ~$800M was one customer;
  Middle East is a big region) create air-pockets when they roll off or get disrupted.
- **Working-capital intensity** — big contracts tie up receivables for a long time (Mexico
  receivable was ~$190M at June 2026, collected over a year+).
- **Healthcare drag** until it recovers.
- **Margin mix volatility** quarter-to-quarter (product vs service, which division, which geography).
