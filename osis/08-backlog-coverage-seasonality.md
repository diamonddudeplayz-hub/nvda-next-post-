## Coverage ratio (backlog ÷ LTM revenue) — the key signal
- Sat flat at **~1.05–1.08x for six straight quarters**, then **jumped to 1.16x in Q1 FY27** — a
  multi-year high.
- **Why it matters:** coverage = how much forward revenue is already contracted. Rising coverage means
  **backlog is growing FASTER than revenue** → forward visibility is *improving*, not just holding.
  1.16x = ~14 months of revenue already in hand. For a lumpy government-contract business, **>1x and
  rising is a genuinely strong, objective signal** — it's the quantitative proof the backlog story is
  real, independent of management's words. This is the single best "floor is strengthening" data point.
- The jump was driven by the **1.4 book-to-bill** in Q1 FY27 (the $583M bookings quarter).

## Book-to-bill read
Consistently ≥1.0 (orders ≥ revenue → backlog builds). The **1.4 in Q1 FY27** is the standout. The
**0.98 in Q4 FY26** is NOT a bad quarter — Q4 is the seasonally *largest* revenue quarter, so booking
above 1x against that high base is hard, and Middle East booking deferrals weighed. Backlog still held
$1.9B. Context matters: a sub-1 in the biggest quarter ≠ weakness; a 1.4 in the smallest quarter = real strength.

**OSIS is Q4-heavy** (like Itron/software names) — driven by its **June fiscal year-end push + the US
government Sept-30 year-end**. Implications:
- Q1 (which they just reported, Oct 29) looking "light" at $404M is **normal seasonality**, not weakness.
- The "**2H FY27 ramp**" management keeps citing is *partly just seasonality* (Q3/Q4 are always the big
  quarters) **layered on top of** backlog conversion. So the inflection claim is less heroic than it
  sounds — some of it is the normal calendar.
- Read each print against its seasonal norm, not the prior quarter.
