# OSIS — Testing "Backlog Accrual = Value Accrual" + the Reverse-DCF Math

*Original empirical work. Answers two questions: (1) is rising backlog actually rising value, or just
a number? (2) how was the "~4% implied growth" derived? As of 10 Nov 2026.*

## The claim
"Backlog accruing is value accruing, whether or not the market claps." Testable — here's the data.

## Test 1 — Does backlog convert to revenue? (backlog vs NEXT-year revenue)
| FYE backlog | → next-yr revenue | ratio |
|---|---|---|
| FY21 $1.08B | FY22 $1.18B | 0.91x |
| FY22 $1.20B | FY23 $1.28B | 0.94x |
| FY23 $1.80B | FY24 $1.54B | 1.17x |
| FY24 $1.70B | FY25 $1.71B | 0.99x |
| FY25 $1.80B | FY26 $1.79B | 1.01x |

**Backlog ran ~1.00x next-year revenue every year (range 0.91–1.17). $1 of backlog ≈ $1 of revenue ~12
months later — reliable, not phantom.**

## Test 2 — Do backlog, revenue, EPS grow together? (FY21→FY26 CAGR)
Backlog **+12%/yr** · Revenue **+9%/yr** · EPS **+14%/yr** → compounded in lockstep. Value tracked the
order book.

## Test 3 — The mechanism on the tape
Backlog surged **+50% (FY22→FY23)**, revenue surged **+20% one year later (FY23→FY24)**. The build led
the conversion by ~a year.

## Verdict + the one caveat
**TRUE for OSI, empirically: backlog→revenue is ~1:1 and reliable over 5 years.** CAVEAT: backlog→
*earnings* (value) also depends on the **margin** it converts at. Revenue conversion is proven; margin
is the second variable (why we underwrite flat margins and watch GM). Further tests: cancellation rate
(10-K: firm orders, minimal cancellations), book-to-bill lead, conversion-margin stability.

## The reverse-DCF — CORRECTED to 2-stage (single-stage was misleading)
**WACC ≈ 9.0%** (Ke 11.3% CAPM = 5% Rf + 1.26β × 5% ERP; Kd 1.7% after-tax on cheap 0.5%/2.25% converts;
76/24 E/D weights).

⚠️ **The single-stage Gordon model ("implied g = WACC − FCF/EV = 4%") is WRONG to cite as "pessimistic"**
— 4% *perpetual* growth is ~GDP, a normal terminal assumption; the model conflates near-term and terminal.

**Proper 2-stage** (stage-1 growth for 5yr → 3% terminal, 8.8% WACC, FCF₀ ~$190M):
| Stage-1 growth | Fair EV | vs actual $3,841M |
|---|---|---|
| 5%/yr | $3,680M | −4% (rich) |
| **6%/yr** | ≈ today | **fair — what's priced** |
| 7%/yr | $4,008M | +4% (cheap) |
| 9%/yr | $4,361M | +14% (cheap) |

**The price implies ~6% stage-1 growth — the LOW END of the 6–9% consensus, NOT 4%.** So: modestly cheap
IF OSI delivers mid-to-high consensus (7–9%, which the $2.1B backlog supports), roughly fair at 5–6%. The
honest claim for the post: **"the market prices ~6%, the low end of its own consensus"** — a modest edge
contingent on conversion, not a screaming mispricing.

## What $2.1B backlog implies (forward)
Backlog ~1.0x next-yr revenue → **$2.1B now → ~$2.0–2.1B following-year revenue ≈ FY28 consensus $2.04B**
(slightly above). The orders to hit consensus FY28 already exist; rising backlog supports the 7–9% upside case.

## The sharpest framing: the service recovery is UN-PRICED (the variant perception)
- **Margin assumption underneath every number here: FLAT (~16% EBITDA / ~10% FCF margin)** — matches
  consensus. No margin expansion underwritten. Expansion = upside; erosion = downside.
- **Management explicitly guided service back to double-digit** (Alan Edrick, Q1 FY27 call): *"strong
  double-digit growth in FY27 service revenues... doubling, if not more than doubling, in Q2."*
- **But the market prices the median multiple (17x) and consensus models FLAT margins** — i.e. **neither
  the multiple nor the Street's margins price the service recovery.** The market is on "show me" after
  being burned (service decelerated 13%→5%→4%, breaking the old 34x premium).
- **So if service recovers as guided, it's largely un-priced → it re-rates:** margins beat flat consensus
  (estimate revisions up) + the premium narrative reignites (multiple up). You get paid twice.
- **The two re-rate tiers:** to ~20x (median+) needs only revenue conversion (service-independent); to the
  old 25-34x premium needs service back to double-digit (guided, not priced).
- **Honest nuance:** the market's skepticism is RATIONAL (service must be *shown*, not promised). The edge
  is not "market is wrong" — it's **"market prices 'show me,' and the backlog/warranty-roll-off/visibility
  support the show-me getting delivered."**

**One-line thesis:** *Priced at the median multiple on "show me" skepticism about a service recovery
management guided and the Street hasn't put in margins — if the Jan/Apr prints show it, the recovery is
un-priced on both estimates and multiple, so it re-rates. You're paid the median multiple to wait for a
proof the backlog supports.*
