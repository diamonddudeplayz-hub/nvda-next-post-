## THE DECISION

**BUY a starter (~1/3 of target) now. Scale on confirmation. This is a patient, floored, positively-
skewed bet on funded revenue growth — NOT a table-pound, and NOT a fast trade.**

- **Entry:** starter at ~$196; accumulate in the **$188–209** range; **add hard at the 200-week (~$177)**
  if offered; **scale to full on the Jan (Q2) and April (Q3) prints** confirming the 2H ramp.
- **Stop adding / reassess:** close **below ~$186 on heavy volume after a weak Q2** (service growth NOT
  re-accelerating) → the reset is becoming a stall.
- **Return profile:** base **~+11% over ~1.5yr** (hold 17x, hit FY28 EPS) / bull **+38–45%** (re-rate) /
  bear **−12% to −32%** (stall/de-rate). Probability-weighted EV **~+6–10%**, positively skewed because
  the backlog floors the revenue being amplified.
- **Hold it like an owner:** rising funded backlog = value accruing regardless of conversion timing;
  you're paid ~5.6% FCF yield to wait near a floor; the re-rate is the bonus, not the thesis.

**Equally defensible alternative:** wait for the April print, pay up ~15% if it confirms, trade a
better entry for better information. Both are rational. The ONE thing that is NOT defensible is paying
18–21× before the business earns it.

## THE STEELMANNED BEAR CASE (argued at full strength)

*If I were short this, here's the case — and it's genuinely strong.*

**1. It's a lumpy government hardware contractor cosplaying as a compounder.** Strip the narrative: OSI
sells boxes to governments on unpredictable timelines. Gross margin has *structurally declined* 36%→33%
over the cycle and the Street models it **flat, not recovering**. The vaunted "service annuity" is
thinner than bulls claim — a big chunk of reported "services" is **low-margin installation/turnkey work
attached to hardware deployments**, not high-margin recurring maintenance. Nobody has broken out the
*truly recurring* piece or its margin, because it probably isn't as pretty as the story.

**2. The ENTIRE return requires a re-rate the market may refuse to grant.** The SOTP ≈ the current price.
At 15× (no re-rate), hitting FY28 consensus = **flat**. The market de-rated this for *good reasons*:
lumpy, government-dependent, margin-pressured, 2.6× levered, no dividend. A business like that
**arguably DESERVES 13–15×, not 18–21×.** The bull case is "the market will change its mind" — which is
*precisely the Adobe value-trap-timing risk from Darren's own framework*: an **undated** re-rate you
cannot schedule. You can sit at 17× for two years earning the FCF yield and nothing else.

**3. The catalyst has already slipped TWICE — betting on it is betting the same thing won't fail a third
time.** The "2H ramp" was promised and missed in Q3 FY26 (−1.8%) and *blown up* in Q4 FY26 (−12%). The
easy Q3/Q4 FY27 comps that make the ramp "achievable" exist **precisely because management already
missed.** Management has a *pattern* of guiding to a back-half recovery that geopolitics or procurement
derails. "This time the backlog converts" is a hope, and the base rate on that hope is 0-for-2 recently.

**4. The operating leverage is currently cutting the WRONG way.** Q1 FY27 showed **negative** operating
leverage — revenue +5% but operating income *declined*. The $368M cost base grew 51% and is committed.
If revenue stalls (a third Middle East slip, CBP procurement delay, a Mexico air-pocket), **the leverage
reverses violently** and EPS falls even with buybacks. The whole bull case assumes volume shows up; if
it doesn't, you don't get flat — you get margin *compression* on a fixed cost base.

**5. "Locked funding" is agency money, not OSI revenue.** IDIQ ceilings ($200M/$85M) are **not spend** —
the $85M had a $21M initial order; the $200M disclosed none. The appropriations ($3.45B Secure America
NII + $6.168B broad OBBBA CBP) are for the *agency*, across many vendors and uses. And the **GAO
documented OSI's own scanners sitting unused in storage** — more money ≠ faster deployment ≠ OSI
revenue. The FY28 ramp everyone underwrites is a year+ out and hostage to government execution with a
*documented history of delay*.

**6. The EPS growth is financially engineered.** Q1's +17% EPS was buybacks + lower interest, **not
operations**. The buyback is partly funded by the **one-time Mexico cash harvest, which ENDS** — so FCF
steps down FY27→FY28 ($209M→$162M) and the buyback decelerates. Strip the engineering and the *organic
operating* growth is pedestrian — mid-single-digit revenue with *declining* gross margin.

**7. No edge: thin coverage BUT crowded ownership.** Only 2 analysts, but it's **~90% institutionally
owned** (BlackRock 14%, Fidelity 10%, Vanguard 12%) — the smart money already owns it at this price.
There is **zero informational edge** here. You're taking a pure multiple view on a name the institutions
have already priced. If it were obviously cheap, they'd have bid it to 20× already — they haven't.

**8. The tails are fat and real.** Healthcare is a persistent loss-making drag with no fix until FY28+.
Nuctech/China caps international growth. And "deferred, not lost" becomes "lost" if the Middle East
escalates — not even the nuclear tail, just *prolonged* conflict leading to eventual cancellations. The
downside if the April ramp fails AND margins contract is **−32% to the Aug low / 200-week**.

**Bear's verdict:** *A fairly-valued, margin-declining, government-timing-dependent box-seller whose
entire upside is an undated re-rate the market has no obligation to grant, whose catalyst has failed
twice, whose EPS growth is buyback sugar, and which the smart money already owns. 15× is fair. There is
no margin of safety — just a story about next year, again.*

## THE BULL REBUTTAL (point by point)

1. **Lumpy box-seller?** Partly — but a **regulation-moated #1** in cargo with 12% ROIC, expanding
   *operating* margin (8%→12.6% via real operating leverage), and a service mix that *is* growing, even
   if slowly. The moat is not fake; Nuctech is *banned* from its core market.
2. **Needs a re-rate?** Yes — but it **traded 24–34× within 18 months**, so the re-rate isn't
   hypothetical; the market has a *demonstrated* willingness to pay up for this exact name. And at a held
   17× you still make ~+11% on EPS growth alone — the re-rate is upside, not a prerequisite for a return.
3. **Slipped twice?** True — but Q1 FY27 was **clean (beat)**, deliveries are resuming, and the misses
   were a *specific, external, temporary* cause (an active war zone), not a demand or execution failure.
4. **Negative leverage now?** In the *seasonally weakest* quarter with the *worst mix* — the Street models
   margin ramping 13%→18.6% within the year as volume builds. Q1 is not the read.
5. **Agency money, not revenue?** Correct it's not guaranteed — but it's **appropriated, multi-year, and
   Democrat-proof**, OSI is a preferred/leading vendor, and RPO ($2.0B, 64% converting <12mo) is
   *contracted*, not hoped-for. The demand visibility is unusually strong for an industrial.
6. **Engineered EPS?** The *buyback* is engineered, yes — but it's funded by **real cash** at prices
   *above* today ($204), and the *operating* leverage is real over the cycle, contingent on the volume
   the backlog should deliver.
7. **No edge?** Correct — the edge isn't information, it's **duration/patience**: willing to hold a
   floored, funded compounder through a re-rate the market is currently too impatient to pay for.
8. **Fat tails?** Real, but **low-probability** (−32% at ~13%), cushioned by backlog/RPO/FCF-yield/floor.

## HOW TO KNOW WHO'S RIGHT (the resolver)
It all collapses to the **April 2027 (Q3 FY27) print.** The bear wins if: revenue misses the +10% bar,
operating margin stays flat/down, service growth stays single-digit, or the Middle East slips a 3rd
time. The bull wins if: revenue hits/beats +10%, **operating margin finally ramps with volume**, service
goes double-digit, and backlog/RPO hold. **Everything before April (incl. the low-bar Jan Q2 beat) is
noise; April is the verdict.** Size the position so you're comfortable being wrong until then.

## FINAL WORD
Good business, fair price, real moat on regulation / contested on competition, funded and growing
demand, a floor you can see, an undated-but-demonstrated re-rate, and a 0-for-2 catalyst that's now
lapping easy comps. **The honest shape: modest positive EV, positively skewed, downside mostly "wait"
not "lose," resolved in April.** A starter is right. Full size is for after the business earns it.
