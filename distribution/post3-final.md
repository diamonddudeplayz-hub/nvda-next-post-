**Title:** Everyone's comparing gold to 2011 right now. On the metric that actually mattered then, it's the opposite setup.

Gold's down 37% from its January high and just made a new low for the year, so the 2011 comparison is everywhere again. Gold fell about 44% over roughly four years after that peak, paste it onto now and you get something ugly. I'm long gold through WPM, weigh that, but I think the comparison breaks on the one metric that actually drove 2011.

That metric is the 10-year real yield, the return on inflation-protected Treasuries, which is gold's real opportunity cost. The headline fed funds rate tells you nothing about 2011 because it was pinned at zero the whole time. The real yield is what moved: near zero at the 2011 peak, then up about 1.7 points through the 2013 taper tantrum, and gold fell 44% right on cue.

Now run it on this cycle. The real yield rose about 3 points from the 2021 low to the January 2026 peak, a bigger move than 2011, and gold tripled instead of halving. Same direction on the thing that's supposed to cap gold, opposite result. It's around 2.8% now, restrictive, with room to fall when the Fed eventually turns. 2011 had that headwind in front of it. This cycle it's mostly behind.

What inverted it is the buyer. In 2011 the price was set by Western ETFs and macro funds, price-sensitive, so when they sold it fell for years. Central banks were buying then too but were too small to matter. Now they're roughly double the size, around 1,000 tonnes a year, and they don't care about price, they buy to get out of dollars. Q3 just printed a provisional 321 tonnes, above Q2's 296 and the 227 five-year average, about 885 over the trailing four quarters. The price fell this autumn and the buying didn't flinch. They fund it by dumping Treasuries, foreign official holders cut $198B over the year to September.

So I'm not trying to call the bottom. I buy a fixed amount monthly and let the schedule set the entry. What I actually watch is one test: if central banks keep buying above 800 tonnes a year but gold is still below its 200-week average (~$3,000, rising) when the WGC prints Q2 2027 demand, the buying isn't doing what I think and I'm wrong.

Where I'm least sure, and what I'd want this sub to push on: am I leaning too hard on the real-yield story because it's the one that keeps me long? If you think 2011 does rhyme, what are you watching that I'm not, the price itself, or the buyers?
