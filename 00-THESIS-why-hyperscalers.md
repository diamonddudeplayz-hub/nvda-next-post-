# The thesis spine: why own the hyperscalers (Nov 22, 2026)

The connective tissue between the original AI-complex handover ("is AI ROI real / do the end-users keep
paying?") and the GOOGL + AMZN deep-dives. This is *why* the work keeps landing on the scalers.

## The one belief this all rests on
**AI has true, durable usage.** Not a forecast — observable (how you actually use it) and, more
importantly, **contracted**: the hyperscaler backlogs are **multiples of current cloud revenue** —
AWS **$578B** (~3x its run-rate), Google Cloud **$561B** (~4.5x). That's demand *already signed, waiting
for capacity.* Reported cloud growth (+39% AWS, +103% Google) is the ceiling of **how fast they can pour
concrete and plug in chips — not the ceiling of demand.** Capex is exploding ($220B AWS, $217B Google)
to chase demand they already hold under contract, not building on spec. Jensen's framing ("AI = token
factories; a 1 GW factory costs ~$50B and generates ~$40B/yr; agents consume tokens through the roof")
is the *story*; the backlogs are the *proof*. (Weight the backlogs, not the shovel-seller's word.)

## The risk ladder — same demand, three very different wrappers
If AI demand is real, there are three ways to own it, in rising order of safety:

| Vehicle | What it is | The risk |
|---|---|---|
| **The labs** (Anthropic, OpenAI) | the demand *creators* | **Funding risk.** Burn billions, depend on continuous raises, circular financing. The demand is real; *who pays for it* is the question. |
| **Pure neoclouds** (CoreWeave, Nebius) | rent GPUs, nothing else | **Single-threaded + levered + concentrated.** Their *whole business is that one thing*, financed with debt, and concentrated in a handful of lab/hyperscaler customers. Binary — one big customer wobbles, the business wobbles. |
| **The hyperscalers** (GOOGL, AMZN, MSFT) | cloud (demand capture) **+ a profitable base business underneath** | **Cleanest.** The base (Search/ads/retail) self-funds the buildout; the demand is diversified across the economy; and they have an internal demand floor (their own models). |

**The conclusion:** you believe AI has true usage, so you own the demand — but you own it through the
vehicle with the **least financing risk and the most diversification.** That's the hyperscalers. The
labs carry funding risk; the neoclouds are a leveraged bet on a few customers; the scalers are a
**diversified, self-funded bet on AI adoption writ large.** Same demand, cleanest wrapper.

## Why the hyperscaler demand is actually clean — who are they renting to?
The crux: if the scalers were *also* mostly renting to the labs, they'd just be neoclouds with a nicer
logo. They're not. Cloud demand splits three ways:

1. **Enterprises — the bulk (and the key).** Jassy: "the vast majority of our capacity is consumed by
   external customers," with demand "barbelled" — labs on one end, the **huge enterprise middle** on the
   other. 85% of IT is still on-prem, migrating over 10–20 years. Thousands of customers, no single point
   of failure. **This is exactly what the neoclouds lack.**
2. **AI labs — a meaningful slice, but diluted.** Anthropic runs on *both* Google TPU and Amazon
   Trainium; OpenAI on Azure/AWS/Oracle. But for a hyperscaler the labs are a *slice* of a $120–184B book,
   not the whole thing (vs ~60%+ customer concentration at a neocloud). **Same customer, totally different
   concentration risk.**
3. **Themselves — the demand floor.** Google fills capacity with Gemini/Search/YouTube; Amazon with
   Alexa/Nova/retail/ads. If external demand ever softened, they can **redirect capacity to their own
   models** — a floor neoclouds don't have, with zero counterparty/funding risk (it's their own P&L).

**On circular financing:** the circular risk (financier invests in the customer who buys from the
financier) is concentrated in the **shovel (NVDA) and the neoclouds.** For hyperscalers it's a *trace* —
yes, Amazon/Google invested in Anthropic and Anthropic buys their compute, but it's a small fraction of a
diversified book, dwarfed by the enterprise base, and **Anthropic's IPO de-risks the funding** (public
capital, not just the cloud provider's). Trace dose, not the full dose.

## The honest caveat (where even this breaks)
The hyperscaler demand is *diversified away from the fragile counterparties* — it is **not risk-free.**
The real second-order risks:
1. **Does enterprise AI ROI hold?** If a broad AI-ROI disappointment hit *enterprises* (not just labs),
   enterprise cloud spend slows — and that's a bigger pool than the labs. This is the original handover
   question, just moved up the stack from "do the labs pay" to "do the enterprises keep paying."
2. **Does pricing/margin compress when supply catches up?** Today demand > supply (the good regime). When
   the memory fabs and data centers catch up, the question is whether pricing normalizes.
3. **Does the capex earn its return?** The backlogs say demand is there; the ROIC has to convert.
But notice the *first-order* question — "is there demand" — the backlogs answer **yes.** The debate is
about the *durability of the ROI*, not the *existence of the demand.* That's a much better place to be
than the labs (funding) or the neoclouds (leverage + concentration).

## How this ties back to the handover
The handover's one question was *"do the end-users keep paying for the compute?"* The hyperscaler
backlogs ($578B / $561B of *contracted* demand) are the strongest single piece of evidence that the
answer is **yes, and it's prepaid.** And the scalers are the way to own that answer **without** taking the
labs' funding risk or the neoclouds' leverage. **GOOGL and AMZN are the two cleanest listed expressions;
own the cloud + base-business compounders, not the shovel (NVDA) or the levered single-threaded rentals.**

_Vehicles ranked for this portfolio: hyperscalers (core/ballast) > NVDA (small torque stub) >> neoclouds
(binary, avoid as core) and labs (not directly investable / funding risk)._
