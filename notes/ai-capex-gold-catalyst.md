Parked idea, not drafted. The hook: the AI capex cycle is the swing factor for the rate path, and the rate path is gold's near-term driver. So the AI build-out and the gold thesis are the same macro trade seen from two ends. ## The core argument

- **Near term, AI capex is a gold headwind.** Hyperscaler/neocloud borrowing + Treasury supply keeps the long end near 5% (the fiscal-dominance leg). High rates = the financial-layer pressure on gold right now.
- **AI de-grossing is already in the drawdown.** The WGC blamed gold's October fall partly on "speculative de-grossing" and an equity risk-off rotation into bonds. When the AI trade wobbles, liquid assets (gold included) get sold for cash.
- **The catalyst is the turn.** Gold's upside leg comes when the Fed pivots to cuts, and the Fed cuts when the economy slows / inflation cools. An **AI capex slowdown** is a prime route to that slowdown. So:
  - AI boom / capex keeps rising → higher-for-longer → gold headwind persists, you keep accumulating.
  - AI capex slows or busts → equity de-rate, slowdown, unemployment up → **Fed cuts → gold re-rates.**
- **Therefore WPM-on-a-schedule partially hedges an AI equity book.** The thing that hurts the tech trade (capex disappointment) is the thing that lights gold's fuse. That's the sharp, non-obvious line.
- **My angle (to develop):** the "higher-for-longer" leg assumes capex keeps *rising*. If capex is already rolling over (SemiAnalysis cut), the turn could come sooner than the FedWatch path implies, which pulls gold's catalyst forward. Build the capex-trajectory case.

## Warsh framing (Fed Chair, "Opening Remarks," Nov 16 2026, SF)

Three illustrative staff scenarios, which map onto the rate path:
1. SI lifts productivity, gains shared → real GDP persistently above trend, near full employment → **higher-for-longer.**
2. SI expansion + labor displacement → GDP up but unemployment above full-employment → mixed.
3. SI disappoints → "equity valuations could fall, leading to an economic slowdown and a more notable rise in unemployment," recovery later without the productivity gains → **the cuts scenario.**

Use scenario 3 as the explicit bridge to the gold catalyst.

## Honesty flags for when I draft this

- Don't conflate a *forecast* cut with *actual* spend: NVDA reaffirmed/raised its own 75% and commitments rose. The bear signal is the out-year (cal-2028) commitments and DSO, not the near-term print.
- One research firm's cut isn't a trend; pair it with the receivables/DSO and neocloud-financing evidence before calling a capex rollover.
- Symmetry test: if capex re-accelerates (Anthropic IPO reopens lab financing), the thesis says higher-for-longer and gold's catalyst is pushed out, not that gold falls apart. Say that.
