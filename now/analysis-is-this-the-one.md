# NOW at ~$102: "is this the one?" (Nov 7, 2026)

Analysis, not advice. Darren's instinct: the cRPO scare is mostly accounting, the stock
fell ~40% in a year, and this is the most interesting name in the set. Mostly right.

## The setup
A ~20% grower with **98% renewal**, non-GAAP op margins ~32%, **36% FCF margin guide**, and
real AI traction (AI ACV +65%, raised to $1.7B) — trading at **22x NTM P/E / 6.3% FCF yield**,
down from **50x** a year ago. The −27% Q3 drop was triggered by an optical cRPO decel that
all three major banks call mostly accounting/timing.

## On the cRPO question specifically (Darren's "just accounting, no?")
**Substantially yes** — and the Street agrees:
- The Q4 cRPO guide (18.5% vs ~21% Q3) is mostly **early-renewal pull-forward (timing)** + **T4C exclusion (~50bps)** + **on-prem mix (~25bps)** + **PS carve-out (~50bps)** — all of which hit the *leading indicator* but **not revenue**. Subscription revenue beat and the full-year guide was **raised**.
- JPM: the decel is "largely on account of revenue drivers moving out of scope of cRPO… on a temporary basis." TD Cowen: "model noise." BofA: "partially attributed to accounting."
- **The honest caveat:** even stripping all that out, organic growth is decelerating *a little* at scale (FY27 sub growth ~18.9% per JPM), and BofA won't fully absolve management ("growing hiccups"). So it's **mostly, not entirely, accounting.**

Net: the 27% one-day crash on a noisy optical metric, while revenue beat and FCF-margin
guide went *up*, looks like an overreaction. That's the bull case in one sentence.

## Why NOW is genuinely the most interesting name in the set
1. **The de-rate is already done.** NVDA/Google are still arguing about *whether* to de-rate on capex/Search. NOW already fell ~40% and halved its multiple. You're buying after the repricing, not before.
2. **It's the anti-capex name.** 6.3% FCF yield, **capex being cut**, 36% FCF margins — the opposite of Google's negative NTM FCF. In a market suddenly punishing cash-burn, NOW *generates* cash. That's a real differentiator against the whole AI-infra complex.
3. **Same core question as Google/Search, cleaner.** Is AI accretive or cannibalistic to the incumbent? NOW's answer (JPM's words): it's the **"system of action… positioned to benefit from agentic AI rather than be disintermediated by it.**" If that's right, 22x is cheap.

## The bear case (be fair — this is why KeyBanc has a lone Sell)
1. **"AI disrupts SaaS" is a real risk, not just sentiment.** If vibe-coding / the labs' own agents / AI-native startups let enterprises assemble workflows without ServiceNow, or if **seat compression outruns consumption growth**, terminal value is impaired. This is the Citrini "destructive disruption in SaaS showing up in the numbers" bucket. NOW is the poster child for that fear.
2. **The offset hasn't shown up where it counts.** AI ACV is booming in *bookings color* (ACV, deal counts) but **cRPO — the top-line leading indicator — is decelerating, not inflecting.** The "consumption flywheel reaccelerates us in 2027" is management's promise, not yet a printed number. "This is the one" requires underwriting that 2027 reacceleration.
3. **Maturing grower.** 50x→22x can be a *correct* re-rating of a company going from hyper-growth to ~18–20% at scale. 22x isn't dirt cheap if growth keeps fading; it's cheap only if AI reaccelerates it.
4. **Promotional management.** McDermott's style (serial AI-target raises, "one-of-one," "Instagram") cuts both ways — confidence or spin.

## My read
Darren's cRPO call is correct and backed by all three banks: **mostly accounting/timing,
revenue intact, FCF guide up — the crash overshot.** And NOW is the sharpest "fallen-angel
quality compounder" in the set because the de-rate is done and it's cash-generative while
everything else burns.

**But "this is the one" rests on one belief:** that AI is *accretive* to ServiceNow (the
Control Tower / consumption story reaccelerates cRPO in 2027), not cannibalistic. The bull
evidence is bookings (AI ACV +65%); the missing evidence is the leading indicator (cRPO
still decelerating). So the trade is: **a cheap, cash-generative compounder where the AI
monetization is visibly starting but hasn't yet hit the headline growth line — and the
market is pricing the bad outcome.** If you believe the 2027 reaccel, the risk/reward is
the best in the group. If AI structurally lowers what enterprises pay for workflow
software, 22x is a value trap — same shape as the Google-Search call, just further along.

### The pair logic
NOW slots next to Google as the two "AI-accretive-or-cannibalistic to a software incumbent"
bets — but NOW is the **cash-generative, already-de-rated** version, where Google carries
the capex *and* the Search risk. If you want the software-incumbent exposure without the
capex arms race, NOW is the cleaner expression; if you want the one the market has already
given up on, same answer.

### What to watch
- **Q4 cRPO print (late Jan):** does organic cRPO stabilize/inflect, or keep sliding? This is the single tell on whether the "2027 reaccel" is real.
- **AI ACV → cRPO conversion:** the moment consumption shows up in the leading indicator, the de-rate reverses hard.
- **Seat metrics / net revenue retention:** evidence on whether consumption is offsetting seat compression.
- **KeyBanc's thesis:** if the lone Sell starts getting company, the structural-disruption fear is winning.
