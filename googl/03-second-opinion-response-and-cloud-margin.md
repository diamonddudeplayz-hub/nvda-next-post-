Darren got an external critique (research 8.5 / thesis 8 / valuation-risk 6.5). **It's a strong,
largely correct review — I'm conceding most of it.** Below: what it got right, where the data it cites

## 1. Where the critique is RIGHT (concede these)
1. **"Self-funds from a 40% ad margin" is overstated.** Correct — and important. 9mo'26 **FCF was
   −$4.8B**; Alphabet **raised ~$147B** (debt +$78.6B, common +$42.9B, preferred +$19.1B) and **halted
   buybacks** to fund the buildout. It is *financed by* a profitable franchise + fortress balance
   sheet + external capital — **not FCF-self-funding today.** Also the 40.7% is Google **Services** op
   margin, not advertising standalone. Fixed in `00-verdict-and-edge.md`.
2. **EV/EBITDA is a weak lens when capex > EBITDA.** This is the best point in the review. At ~10x FY27
   EV/EBITDA the company is spending **~$337B capex against ~$306B EBITDA** — EBITDA ignores the
   capital intensity. The right lenses are **ROIC / incremental operating income per incremental
   invested capital / FCF conversion once assets turn productive.** I leaned too hard on EV/EBITDA.
3. **Risk/reward is ~1.4:1 on midpoints, not 2:1.** Arithmetic: bull mid +40% / bear mid −29% = **1.4:1**.
   My "2:1" only holds if you probability-weight the bear lower. On raw magnitudes the critique is right.
4. **My bear is too mild** — it's a "moderate disappointment" (EPS −13%, −29%), not a true thesis
   failure. A *structural* Search-economics break would hit the $63B/qtr profit pool far harder. A
   proper stress case (Search revenue *declines* + AI-Mode monetizes well below legacy + Cloud margin
   compresses + capex keeps rising) is worse than −30%. Added below.
5. **TPU is not costless.** Confirmed in the 10-Q: **$6.6B maximum potential payments on a TPU-hardware
   *repurchase* guarantee** (Google must buy back sold TPU under certain conditions). It's small vs
   NVDA's financing exposure, and Google says counterparties have no economic incentive to exercise —
   but it proves the "free optionality" framing needs the *incremental economics* worked out, not
   assumed. "Nearly free" → "underappreciated strategic asset whose standalone economics I haven't
   proven."
6. **Don't say the data "disproves" Search displacement.** Fair. Better: *"the demand-side disruption
   is far less evident than the monetization-side challenge."* The share data shows Google hasn't
   suffered a *catastrophic* demand loss — not that competitive displacement can't happen.
7. **The $217B vs $195–205B capex gap needs explaining** (see §3). **Backlog definition changed in
   Q1'26** to include ≤1-yr contracts — flag when comparing backlog growth across periods. Both fair.
8. **2029 FCF inflection is fragile** — a 20% capex overrun erases the +$64B, and +$64B is only ~2%
   FCF yield on EV. Agreed; I'd call 2029 a *consensus hope*, not a dependable recovery.

## 3. Where I'd push back / add nuance (not overturn)
- **The OpenAI 20% number is the softest data in the debate** — self-reported by the competitor, no

  it doesn't flip the conclusion.
- **The TPU-gross-up margin claim is a *well-grounded* hypothesis, not a wild one.** Hardware sold into
  a customer's data center is structurally lower-margin than cloud rental/software — that's basic unit
  economics, not a guess. The critique is right the *filing doesn't quantify it*, so I'll label it a
  hypothesis — but it's a strong prior, and it's the same reason to watch Cloud margin (§4).
- **The $217B vs $195–205B capex gap** — most likely a blend of: (a) TIKR's "Free Cash Flow" nets
  more than PP&E (e.g. finance-lease principal), inflating implied capex; and (b) **consensus models

  times*). So treat FY26 capex as a **~$205–217B range**, not a point. It doesn't change direction but
  the critique is right not to leave it unexplained.
- **We largely AGREE on the forward caution.** My `02-…` doc already said watch Cloud *operating
  dollars not margin %*, flagged the bridging-capacity drag, and called the n=1 segment data thin. The
  critique is mostly sharpening the **headline verdict's confident tone** — which is fair. Net: I move
  my **valuation/entry confidence to *moderate*, competitive-position confidence stays *high*** — the
  same split the critique lands on.

**Bottom line on the review:** it correctly separates *great business* / *great AI business* /

## 4. THE QUESTION: is the Street/price pricing Cloud margin expansion — and how much?
Short answer: **The big Cloud margin expansion already happened and is in the price. Forward, consensus
prices Cloud margin to roughly HOLD the ~36% gains (modest drift to high-30s/low-40s by '28), NOT to
keep surging. The incremental profit the Street models is overwhelmingly REVENUE-driven, and it quietly
assumes margin holds *through* the TPU-hardware mix shift — which is the optimistic part.**

### What already happened (in the actuals, in the price)
Cloud **operating** margin: **~26% (FY25) → 32.9% (Q1'26) → 35.6% (Q2) → 36.2% (Q3'26)** — a ~10–12pt
jump in a year. That's the operating-leverage inflection, and it's **visible/done**, not a forecast.

### What's priced forward (the decomposition)
Caveat: TIKR "EBITDA" doesn't reconcile cleanly to segment operating income (it adds back more than
D&A), so I use **operating income**, which is clean from the 10-Q. Illustrative, transparent:

| | Cloud rev | assumed Cloud op margin | → Cloud op income | % of group op income |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY25 (actual-ish) | $59B | ~26% | ~$15B | ~13% |
| FY26E | $112B | ~36% (holds Q3 exit) | ~$40B | ~24% |
| FY27E | $210B | ~37–40% | **~$78–84B** | ~38% |
| FY28E | $323B | ~38–40% | **~$123–129B** | ~40% |

So consensus implicitly has **Cloud going from ~13% to ~40% of group operating profit by 2028** — an
**~8x rise in Cloud operating income in three years.** But look at the margin column: the Street is
assuming Cloud op margin **holds ~36–40%**, *not* another 12-pt leg. **~90% of the forward Cloud profit
growth is revenue (volume), ~10% is margin.** The n=1 segment-EBITDA data agrees directionally — Cloud
EBITDA margin ~55% ('26) → ~53% ('27, a dip) → ~58% ('28) — i.e. a **2027 wobble then recovery**,
consistent with TPU hardware + bridging capacity diluting before scale re-expands it.

### So, how much expansion is priced — and is it the risk?
- **Priced:** Cloud margin **holds ~high-30s%** on **doubling revenue**. A few points of *group*
  EBITDA-margin expansion (~43.5% '25 → ~51% '28) comes mostly from Cloud *scale* + Services leverage,
  not from Cloud margin ripping further.
- **NOT priced (downside):** a Cloud margin **dip** if TPU *hardware* (lower margin) + 3rd-party
  bridging + surging D&A compress the operating line in 2027. **If Cloud op margin fell ~5pts in '27
  (e.g. 37%→32% on hardware mix), that's ~$10B less op income ≈ ~$0.65 / ~4% off FY27 EPS, plus a
  sentiment hit on "Cloud margin rolled over."** That's the specific, underappreciated near-term risk —
  and it's exactly the critique's "don't extrapolate 36.2%" point, quantified.
- **NOT priced (upside):** margin re-accelerating to mid-40s+ as own-silicon economics + software mix
  outrun the hardware drag. That's the free call option.

**The clean way to say it:** **the price pays for Cloud *revenue* compounding at a roughly *stable*
high-30s margin — it is NOT paying for continued rapid margin expansion, and it IS (quietly) assuming
margin doesn't break on the 2027 TPU-hardware ramp.** So the thing to watch isn't "does Cloud margin
keep expanding" (it mostly doesn't need to) — it's **"does Cloud margin HOLD through the hardware mix
shift."** That's why, per the critique, the right metric is **incremental Cloud operating income ÷
incremental Cloud capital employed (ROIC)**, not revenue growth or a extrapolated 36.2%. If incremental
Cloud ROIC stays well above cost of capital as TPU systems ramp, the thesis is intact; if the hardware
mix drags incremental returns down, the Cloud leg is worth less than the revenue line implies — and the
$265 price, which pays for ~$125B of Cloud op income by '28, would be discounting too much.

_Net: the Street is pricing Cloud margins to **hold, not expand** — modest group-margin expansion, all
revenue-led. The asymmetric risk is a 2027 margin dip on TPU hardware the consensus isn't modeling; the
asymmetric upside is own-silicon economics re-expanding margin. Track Cloud **operating margin and
incremental ROIC**, not revenue._
