# Response to the red-team (Nov 22, 2026)

The commissioned red-team (`_redteam_*`) is strong and partly right. This is the honest reply: concede
what it won, defend what holds. Bottom line: **it wins the DCF and the kill-switch; it does not win the
EBITDA argument or the discount-rate choice — and those two are where the actual call lives.**

## What the red-team WON (conceded, no spin)
1. **The kill-switch watches the wrong gauge.** This is its best finding and it's correct. "Global share
   rolls over" is a *volume* tripwire; the thesis is about *RPM*. Q3 shows sessions +36% vs paid clicks
   +9–10% — RPM-per-session falling **inside Google's own funnel**, and Services op margin printed 40.7%
   vs ~43.5% expected. **The right gauges are the sessions-vs-paid-clicks spread + Google Services
   operating margin, not Statcounter share.** The capstone kill-switch is now **superseded** — track those
   two. Full concede.
2. **The Search RPM risk is *already materializing*, not future optionality.** The bull chain treated
   monetization as "a lever not yet pulled"; Q3 says it's a present headwind (AI Overviews at 79% of US
   results, ad coverage/CTR down). I under-weighted that it's happening *now*. Concede.
3. **The DCF leaned on the favorable capex file + a sub-CAPM rate.** The $339 "base" sits at the corner of
   the two most generous choices. The honest DCF center is lower (~$250–285). The DCF does **not** prove
   cheapness. Concede — and note I'd already called the DCF "inconclusive / terminal-dominated" in 04, so
   this confirms rather than overturns that: **the DCF was never the load-bearing argument.**

## Where the red-team OVERREACHES (the defense)

### Defense 1 — "CAPM says 11%, so 11% is the honest rate" is itself a choice, and an aggressive one
The red-team anchors its "fairly valued" conclusion on an 11% discount rate and calls it "the rate the
thesis itself derived." But 11% is the *bear* end, not neutral truth:
- **Net cash lowers the effective beta.** The 1.23 is a *levered/trailing* beta; GOOGL carries ~$115B net
  cash (negative net debt), so its *asset* beta is lower. A net-cash franchise doesn't deserve its levered
  beta's equity cost. Adjust → ~1.0–1.1 → ~10–10.4%.
- **The ERP is an assumption.** 5% is on the high side; 4.5% (common) gives ~10.4% even at β1.23.
- **You discount a decade — use a normalized rf, not today's elevated 4.9% spot.** If rates mean-revert
  over the hold, the average discount rate is below the spot-implied 11%.
- **Practitioner norm for quality mega-caps is 8–10%, not 11%.** 11% is defensible as a *conservative*
  case; it is not "the" rate.
So the red-team picking 11% is the mirror image of me picking 10% — both are choices. The honest treatment
is a **range**: at 10% it's modestly cheap, at 11% fairly valued. Your instinct ("CAPM at 11%?") is right
to push back — 11% is the aggressive end for a net-cash name, and leaning the whole "it's fairly valued"
verdict on it is as much a thumb on the scale as my 10% was.

### Defense 2 — the red-team attacked the DCF; the anchor was always EV/EBITDA (your point, and it's the key one)
This is the crux. **~95% of the red-team's firepower hits the DCF** — capex file, discount rate, terminal
sensitivity. But the DCF was *already conceded inconclusive*; I explicitly pivoted to **EV/EBITDA as the
robust anchor** precisely because it sidesteps those two attacks:
- **EV/EBITDA doesn't use the capex file** (EBITDA is before capex). The red-team's single biggest lever —
  the $80B/yr capex-file disagreement — **does not touch it.**
- **EV/EBITDA isn't discounted** — the 10% vs 11% WACC fight **doesn't touch it either.**
- The argument is *relative/historical*: **10.3x FY27 EV/EBITDA vs GOOGL's own ~14–16x history (froth
  18x), vs MSFT 15x, on consolidated EBITDA compounding ~31%/yr** (FY26 $231B → FY27 $306B → FY28 $399B,
  n=23–25, the *reliable* part of the estimate set — not the thin out-year capex the DCF leans on).

**The ONLY way the red-team dents the EBITDA case is via Search RPM impairing EBITDA growth** — its Attack
2. So test that directly: does a Search bear break the EBITDA multiple?
| FY27 EBITDA scenario | EBITDA | EV/EBITDA at $265 |
|---|---|---|
| Consensus | $306B | 10.3x |
| Moderate Search bear (−$17B OI, red-team's X=50%/r=30%) | ~$289B | ~10.9x |

**Even in the red-team's *severe* Search bear, GOOGL is ~12x EV/EBITDA — still below its ~14–16x history
and below MSFT's 15x.** Why? Because **consolidated EBITDA is diversified**: Cloud compounding ~90% offsets
a Services/Search dent, so group EBITDA keeps growing even if Search RPM falls. The red-team's Search model
is a *Services/OI* model — it's real, but at the *consolidated EBITDA* level (what you actually pay the
multiple on) the Cloud ramp cushions it. **So the EBITDA cheapness has a built-in margin of safety to the
exact risk the red-team identifies** — that's the defense the red-team never addressed because it stayed
on DCF ground.
