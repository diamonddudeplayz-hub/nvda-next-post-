**Read:** OCF is *accelerating* (16%→41%/yr). FCF is negative **purely because of capex**,
not because the business stopped minting cash. Consensus models cumulative FCF of roughly
**−$113B across 2026–28**, then a violent inflection to **+$64B (29) → +$141B (30)**.
So the entire bull/bear split on "is GOOGL cheap?" is a timing argument: P/E says 17.8x
(cheap), FCF yield says you get **no owner cash for ~3 years**. Both are true. The gap is capex,
and whether the 2029+ inflection consensus already pencils in actually shows up.
Capex consensus (derived): **~$217B (26) → ~$337B (27) → ~$396B (28)** — vs the
company's FY26 guide of $195–205B and "significant increase" for 2027. The Street is modeling
2027 capex up ~60%+. That is the single most aggressive assumption in the model.

**Caveat that matters:** Cloud's modeled jump to **$323B by 2028 (5.5x 2025)** is turbocharged

are single-analyst (n=1), so treat as directional only.

## 7. What the estimate set tells you in three lines
1. **Profit (EBITDA/op income) compounds low-30s%**; the clean multiple (10x FY27 EV/EBITDA,
   17.8x FY27 P/E) is the cheapest of the quality hyperscalers — *if* you trust the Services line.
2. **FCF is a 3-year desert by design** (capex ~$217B→$337B→$396B); you are underwriting a 2029
   inflection to +$64–141B. The number most likely to be wrong (either way) is 2027 capex.
3. **Growth mix has shifted to Cloud/TPU**, but a chunk of Cloud's headline growth is low-margin
   hardware gross-up — so the top-line optics are better than the margin reality. Judge it on
   segment operating margin, not revenue.
