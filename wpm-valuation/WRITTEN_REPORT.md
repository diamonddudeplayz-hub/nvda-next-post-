## RECOMMENDATION
We initiate on Wheaton Precious Metals with a **BUY** and a **$133 DCF target, +8.5%** above the $122.60 close. The modest base upside is deliberate: our base case prices gold at $4,200/oz, above today's ~$3,530 spot but below the street's $4,500 to $5,200 decks. The case is the **asymmetry**. The bull deck ($5,000 gold) implies **$179 (+46%)**, the bear deck ($3,400 gold) implies **$90 (-26%)**, and WPM is the lowest-risk equity vehicle for a gold thesis we believe the market is mispricing. We would accumulate into the current weakness.

## COMPANY INFORMATION
WPM is the world's largest **precious-metals streaming** company: it pays a miner an upfront deposit for the right to buy a fixed share of future production at a low, pre-set price (~20% of spot). It operates no mines, which is the moat: no mine-level operating, cost-inflation, or capex risk. Its cost is contractually set near **$600 per gold-equivalent ounce (GEO)** (a fixed amount or a low share of spot), so WPM earns roughly **75% gross and 84% EBITDA margins** with about 47 employees; management put Q1-26 gold margins *"close to 86%... you don't see that with producers."* The book spans **45 agreements over 57 assets (22 operating mines) in 19 countries**, with ~75% of production from investment-grade counterparties and ~90% parent-guaranteed. Reserve life tops **60 years**, and $1.45B of net debt swings to **net cash by 2028**. Revenue is split almost evenly between gold and silver [EXHIBIT: revenue-by-metal donut].

## INDUSTRY OVERVIEW
Royalty and streaming companies are mining's capital-light financiers; the peers are Franco-Nevada, Royal Gold, and Triple Flag. Their margins are immune to the cost inflation crushing producers, whose all-in sustaining costs hit a **record $1,930/oz in 2026 (+22% y/y)**. The market pays streamers **2 to 3 times the miners' multiple** (Barrick ~8.6x earnings, Agnico ~13x, WPM ~24x).

## INVESTMENT THESIS: *what the market is missing*
**1. The marginal buyer changed in 2022, so the floor changed.** For two decades gold's marginal buyer was price-sensitive Western capital trading it against real yields, which capped every rally. After the 2022 freeze of ~$300B of Russian reserves re-rated the dollar as politically contingent, **central banks became the marginal buyer**: price-insensitive, buying on reserve mandates, clearing excess supply regardless of price. The floor is a **flow, the pace of that buying** (~885t over the trailing four quarters, 321t in Q3-26, +42% y/y), not a line in the spot price.

**2. The selloff is the price-sensitive layer, and the old rule that governed it broke.** That layer trades gold against the **10-year real yield**, its true opportunity cost; from 2004 to 2021 a 100bp move in real yields drove roughly an 18% move in gold. Gold is down ~18% YTD, yet through this cycle the real yield rose about 3 points, from -1.2% in 2021 to +1.9% at the January 2026 peak and ~2.8% today, a bigger move than 2011's that the old rule says should have **halved gold. Instead it tripled.** Real yields at 2.8% are restrictive with room to fall when the Fed turns, so the selling is a financial-layer headwind, not a verdict on the floor.

**3. The bid is self-reinforcing.** About 38% of buying central banks say they would fund purchases by selling other reserves, typically **Treasuries**; China cut its holdings ~$100B to an 18-year low while adding gold monthly. That selling pressures the long end just as **US net interest expense passed defense spending**, fiscal stress that makes diversifying out of dollars more rational, not less.

**4. Own the streamer, and lower gold does not mean lower value.** Production is **contracted and funded to 1.2M GEO by 2030 (+50%,** six new assets *"all funded and permitted,"* no new capital), so the same multiple on a larger cash-flow base is a higher share price with no re-rating, which is why WPM is roughly flat while gold is down 18%. Silver (~49% of revenue, ~1.6x gold beta) adds torque. The market is extrapolating a depressed spot and under-weighting funded growth.

## VALUATION
We value WPM with a **free-cash-flow-to-equity DCF**, explicit to 2030 (the end of the funded ramp) plus a terminal value. It monetizes the thesis directly: revenue is **volume** (the funded ramp to 1.2M GEO) times **price** (the sensitivity axis, base gold $4,200, silver derived at a 1.63 beta). Because per-GEO cost is fixed, margins expand as price rises. **CAPM gives 10.85% nominal** (Rf 4.95% + beta 1.17 x ERP 5.0%), which we show, but we discount the base at **6% real** on WPM's streaming economics: the model is in constant dollars, so a real rate is the consistent match; WPM's beta reflects gold-price swings over an unusual 2021-26 window, not broad-market risk (gold's own market beta is near zero), and we model that price risk in the scenario decks rather than the rate; and residual risk is investment-grade (net cash by 2028). Our 6% sits above the ~5% streaming and NAV convention and below the **8.15% real CAPM** (10.85% nominal less ~2.5% inflation). Terminal value blends Gordon growth (1% real) and an 18x EV/EBITDA exit. The base yields **$133**, and the FCFF build converges at the same number [EXHIBIT: football-field, bear $90 / base $133 / bull $179].

**The multiple confirms it.** WPM's forward P/OCF has compressed to **~17x, its 2016-18 trough (gold ~$1,250)**, versus a **~24x 2019-24 mid-cycle average**, even as cash flow hit a record and keeps growing. The market is paying a trough multiple on peak, rising cash flow: it is **pricing gold mean-reversion**, the bet our floor thesis rejects. Peers offer no separate bargain, so the upside is the gold thesis, not a cross-sectional re-rate [EXHIBIT: WPM P/OCF vs gold, 2016-26].

## RISKS & SENSITIVITY
The call is a view on two variables, held to falsifiable tests. **Gold price** is the primary axis; a sustained $3,400 deck takes the target to **$90 (-26%)**. We judge a structural reversal unlikely and pre-register falsifiable break conditions: quarterly central-bank demand holding near the 290 to 320t pace (a drop toward Q1's revised 57t would signal normalization), the reserve-funding mix, and China's Treasury-sale pace, none of which has broken. Timing risk is real: gold is at YTD lows and positioning is not washed out. **Discount rate** is the second: on the basis-consistent real CAPM (**8.15% real**, i.e. 10.85% nominal less inflation) the target is **$107, about -13%**. That is the main risk to the base, if the market demands a full equity premium rather than the ~5% streaming rate, though even there the downside is contained. **Silver** (~49% of revenue) is more volatile, and its deficit case is contested.

The **upside risk** is worth naming: three independent methods, the **DCF bull ($179)**, a **multiple re-rate to mid-cycle on 2027E cash flow (~$170)**, and the **street (~$185)**, cluster at **$170 to $185**, so if the floor holds our $133 base will prove conservative.

## CONCLUSION
WPM is the lowest-risk equity way to own a gold thesis: low, fixed-cost cash flows that resist record mining inflation, ~50% of funded volume growth that lifts value even at a lower gold price, and silver torque. The market is pricing a financial-layer selloff as structural while the central-bank bid, now self-reinforced by the fiscal stress it creates, stays intact. **BUY, $133 base (+8.5%)**, asymmetric to **$179 (+46%)**. Buy the vehicle, own the thesis.
