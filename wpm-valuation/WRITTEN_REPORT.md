## Recommendation
We initiate on Wheaton Precious Metals at **BUY**, 12-month target **$146** (FCFE DCF), **+11.4%** above the $131.02 close. The base assumes gold normalizes to $4,500/oz, our base-case deck (roughly 23% above today's ~$3,650 spot and below the street's $4,600 to $5,200), underpinned by the central-bank bid rather than an established price floor. The case is the asymmetry, and the re-rating is already underway: WPM has **outperformed the metal all year (+11% while gold fell -15% and silver -38%) and has firmed further as gold bounced off its November lows**, because the market is pricing a normalized deck and a better business rather than spot. Our bull ($5,200 gold) implies **$189 (+44%)**; our bear ($3,400) implies **$90 (-31%)**. For an investor who wants pure precious-metals exposure with leverage, WPM is the vehicle: it offers the most torque to a gold thesis without a miner's operating risk on the way down.

## Company
WPM is the largest precious-metals streamer: it pays miners an upfront deposit for the right to buy a fixed share of future production at a low, pre-set price (~20% of spot). It operates no mines, so it carries no direct mine-level operating, cost-inflation or sustaining-capex exposure. Cash cost averaged ~$600/GEO in 9M-26 (a reported operating metric, not a single contractual rate), giving **~75% GAAP gross and ~84% EBITDA margins, and a ~86% cash operating margin ex-depletion** (management), with ~47 employees. The book spans **57 assets (22 operating mines) across 19 countries**, ~75% of production from investment-grade counterparties (Vale, Newmont, Glencore) and reserve life beyond 60 years. Production is **contracted and funded to 1.2M GEO by 2030, +50% from 2025**, with management describing the six assets coming online as "all funded and permitted" (the Antamina stream alone is ~18% of 2030 output). No new capital, acquisitions or permitting is required, and the balance sheet reaches net cash by 2028.

## Industry
Royalty and streaming companies finance miners in exchange for a share of production; the peer set is FNV, RGLD and TFPM. Their defining feature is that margins are immune to the cost inflation crushing producers, whose **all-in sustaining costs hit a record $1,930/oz (+22% y/y)** on declining ore grades, deeper mines, and labour and energy inflation. As gold fell ~7% in a recent quarter, miner margins compressed ~16%, downside operating leverage a streamer does not carry. The market pays for the difference: streamers trade at roughly double the producers' EV/EBITDA (**WPM ~19x versus single-digit multiples for large gold producers**), and the gold-heavy royalty leader **FNV trades 20x EV/EBITDA**. FNV is more gold-weighted (~70% gold) and diversified into energy, giving it a lower beta and a defensive profile; WPM is the purer metals play (100% metals, ~49% silver), which gives it the torque, more upside in a gold bull and a deeper, better entry when a washout drags it below fair value.

## Investment thesis
**1. The marginal buyer changed in 2022, so the floor changed.** After the 2022 freeze of ~$300B of Russian reserves re-rated the dollar as a politically contingent asset, central banks replaced price-sensitive Western capital as gold's marginal buyer: price-insensitive, buying on multi-year reserve mandates, clearing the market's excess supply regardless of price. The floor is a *flow*, the pace of that buying (~885t over the trailing four quarters, 321t in Q3-26, +42% y/y), not a line in the spot price.

**2. The selloff is the price-sensitive layer, and the old rule broke.** That layer trades gold against the 10-year real yield, its true opportunity cost; from 2004 to 2021, 100bp of real yield moved gold ~18%. This cycle the real yield rose ~3 points (-1.2% in 2021 to +1.9% at the January-2026 peak, ~2.8% now), a bigger move than 2011's, which the old rule says should have halved gold. It tripled instead. And unlike 2011, when real yields were near zero with room only to rise (a headwind), today they are restrictive with room to fall when the Fed turns (a tailwind), while central-bank buying runs roughly double its 2011 pace.

**3. The bid is self-reinforcing.** In the WGC's 2026 survey, 38% of responding central banks said they would fund new gold purchases by selling existing reserve assets, and about half cited domestic programmes in local currency; the direction of travel is away from traditional reserves. Separately, China has cut its Treasury holdings to a 2008 low ($563.9B in September, down ~$135B y/y) while adding gold nearly every month. Reserve diversification of this kind pressures the long end exactly as **US net interest expense has passed defense spending** (the CBO projects ~$16T of interest over the decade, climbing as yields rise). The 30-year yield sits ~5.3%, near its cycle high. The fiscal stress makes diversification more rational, not less.

**4. Lower gold does not mean lower value.** Production is contracted and funded to 1.2M GEO by 2030, so a constant multiple on a larger, growing cash-flow base is a higher share price with no re-rating required, which is part of why WPM has been resilient as the metal fell. Silver (~49% of revenue, ~1.6x gold beta, correlation 0.79) adds torque; its independent industrial-deficit case is contested, so we lean on silver's monetary, gold-correlated leg and treat the deficit as optionality. The market is extrapolating a depressed spot and under-weighting the funded growth.

## Valuation
Our primary tool is a **5-year FCFE DCF** (explicit to 2030, then terminal value) discounted at a **selected 6% real** cost of equity. We show CAPM (10.85% nominal, 8.15% real; Rf 4.95% + beta 1.18 x ERP 5.0%) as an alternative sensitivity rather than applying it mechanically: at the 8.15% real CAPM the base is **~$118**, and at the full 10.85% nominal rate **~$97**. Our selected 6% reflects the quality and duration of WPM's streaming portfolio: contractual metal entitlements, low ongoing delivery costs, investment-grade counterparties (~75% of production), limited direct mine-level operating and capital exposure, and net cash by 2028. The sector's ~5% stream-level NAV convention is additional context, not directly equivalent to a corporate FCFE discount rate. The 6% is our valuation judgement; the CAPM sensitivities make its impact explicit. The terminal value blends Gordon growth (1% real) and an **18x EV/EBITDA exit, which matches WPM's current trading multiple (18.6x)**, so it does not assume further multiple expansion. Base gold $4,500 yields **$146**; the FCFF build converges.

The multiple corroborates, on two references. Against peers WPM is a deserved premium, not a bargain (peer-median multiples imply ~$103). Against its *own* history it is modestly cheap: its **current-year P/OCF of ~21x (2026E) remains below its ~23x 2019-26 median on the same current-year basis** (and ~18x on next-year cash flow), having compressed with the metal. The stronger evidence is WPM's **relative resilience** while gold and silver corrected: its current-year P/OCF of ~21x (2026E) remains below its ~23x 2019-26 median on the same basis, so the market has not fully restored its historical cash-flow valuation despite the funded growth. This is also why we anchor the base to a normalized deck ($4,500) rather than spot: holding gold flat at today's ~$3,650 is worth only ~$112 in our model, so at our 6% real rate the market is already pricing a deck above spot (equivalently, a higher multiple on current cash flow). Our $4,500 base sits below the street's $4,600 to $5,200 decks.

## Risks & sensitivity
The call is a view on two variables, held to falsifiable tests. **Gold price** is the primary axis; a sustained $3,400 deck takes the target to $90. We judge an *abrupt* reversal of the central-bank bid unlikely; a *gradual* normalization we would monitor through pre-registered break conditions: quarterly central-bank demand holding near the ~300t pace (Q1's soft 57t was distorted by Turkey's lira-defense selling, not a trend break; Q2 was 296t and Q3 321t), and China's continued gold accumulation alongside its Treasury drawdown. None has broken. **Discount rate** is the second axis: on the basis-consistent real CAPM (8.15% real) the target is **$118, about -10%**, the main risk if the market demands a full equity premium rather than the ~5% streaming rate (the full nominal CAPM, 10.85%, implies ~$97). **Silver** (~49% of revenue) is more volatile than gold, and it is the torque that overshoots the stock below fair value on washouts, which is also where the entry is. WPM also bears **partner-execution risk**: it does not operate the mines, so its cash flows depend on counterparties producing and delivering, and the **Antamina stream alone is ~18% of 2030 output**, a concentration to watch. The **upside risk**, and our edge, is that the market is already re-rating WPM (it has been resilient as the metal fell, with P/OCF below its historical median), so if gold merely stabilizes the re-rating can continue: three independent reads, the DCF bull ($189), WPM's own-history median multiple, and the street (~$185), cluster at ~$170 to $189.

## Conclusion
WPM gives leveraged, pure precious-metals exposure to a gold thesis without a miner's operating risk: fixed-cost cash flows that resist record mining inflation, ~50% of funded volume growth that lifts value even at a lower gold price, and silver torque. The market is pricing a financial-layer selloff as structural while the central-bank bid, now reinforced by the fiscal stress that reserve diversification adds to, stays intact, and it has already begun to re-rate a structurally better business. We rate WPM **BUY, target $146 (+11.4%)**, with an asymmetric path to $189.
