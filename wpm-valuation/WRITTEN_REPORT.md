## RECOMMENDATION
We initiate on Wheaton Precious Metals with a **BUY** and a **$133 DCF target (+8.5%** vs the $122.60 close). The headline upside is modest on purpose — our base case prices gold at $4,200/oz, *above* today's ~$3,530 spot but *below* the street's $4,500–5,200 decks. The real case is the **asymmetry**: our bull deck ($5,000 gold) implies **$179 (+46%)**, our bear deck ($3,400 gold) implies **$90 (−26%)**, and WPM is the lowest-risk vehicle for a gold thesis we believe the market is mispricing. We would accumulate on the current gold-driven weakness.

## COMPANY INFORMATION
WPM is the world's largest **precious-metals streaming** company. It pays a miner an upfront deposit for the contractual right to buy a fixed percentage of future production at a low, pre-set price (~20% of spot, or a fixed per-ounce amount). It does not operate mines. That structure is the moat: **no operating costs, no cost inflation, no capex overruns, no single-mine execution risk.** WPM's cost is contractually fixed at ~$600 per gold-equivalent ounce (GEO), so it earns **~75% gross / ~84% EBITDA margins** with ~47 employees — management put Q1-26 gold-side cash margins at *"close to 86%… you don't see that with producers."* The book spans **45 agreements across 57 mines in 19 countries** (22 producing); ~75% of production comes from **investment-grade counterparties** (Vale, Newmont, Glencore) and ~90% carries parent guarantees. Reserve life exceeds **60 years**, and the balance sheet de-levers from $1.45B net debt to **net cash by 2028**. Revenue is split almost evenly between gold and silver [EXHIBIT: revenue-by-metal donut].

## INDUSTRY OVERVIEW
Royalty-and-streaming companies are the capital-light financiers of the mining industry; the peer set is **Franco-Nevada (FNV), Royal Gold (RGLD), and Triple Flag (TFPM)**. The group's defining feature is that its margins are **structurally immune to the cost inflation crushing producers**: global miner all-in sustaining costs hit a **record $1,930/oz in 2026 (+22% y/y)**, and as gold fell ~7% in the quarter, miner margins compressed ~16% — downside operating leverage. Streamers have none of that. The market pays for the difference: **Barrick trades at ~8.6× earnings and Agnico ~13×, versus WPM at ~24×** [EXHIBIT: streamer-vs-miner multiple bar]. Sector demand rests on the gold and silver macro, where a price-insensitive, central-bank-led structural bid is colliding with short-term financial-market volatility.

## INVESTMENT THESIS — *what the market is missing*
**1. The marginal buyer changed in 2022, so the floor changed.** For two decades gold's marginal buyer was Western financial capital trading it against real yields — price-sensitive, it capped every rally (the 2004–21 regression: ~100bp of real yields ≈ an 18% gold move). After the 2022 freeze of ~$300B of Russian reserves re-rated the dollar as a politically contingent asset, **central banks became the marginal buyer** — price-insensitive, buying on multi-year reserve mandates. They now clear the market's excess supply *regardless of price*, so the floor ratchets higher instead of mean-reverting. The floor is therefore a **flow — the pace of this buying — not a line in the spot price**: central-bank demand has run ~1,000t/yr since 2022 (≈2.2× the prior decade), **~885t over the trailing four quarters and 321t in Q3-26 alone (+42% y/y)**.

**2. The selloff is the price-sensitive layer; the floor didn't move.** Gold is down ~18% YTD and ~37% from its January-2026 high, but the World Gold Council attributes the drop to rate repricing and ETF/derivatives-led selling — the *financial layer*. The tell that it isn't structural: the 30-year Treasury yield rose toward ~5.2% while gold *recovered off its lows* and North-American ETFs posted their weakest first half since 2013. Under the old relationship that rate move sends gold lower; **it went up anyway.** Because the correlation broke, we treat this as a *rising-floor* thesis and refuse to forecast a price — we hold gold as our sensitivity axis and set the base deck below the street.

**3. The bid is self-reinforcing.** ~38% of buying central banks fund purchases by **selling other reserve assets, typically Treasuries**; China has cut its Treasury holdings ~$100B to an 18-year low while adding gold nearly every month. That selling pressures the long end exactly as **US net interest expense has surpassed defense spending** (CBO: ~$16T over the decade) — fiscal stress that makes diversifying out of dollars *more* rational, not less. The diversification creates the stress that justifies the diversification.

**4. Own the streamer — and lower gold does not mean lower value.** The fixed ~$600/GEO cost captures gold's upside with a fraction of the downside cost risk now hammering producers, which is why the market pays streamers 2–3× miner multiples; physical gold gives the floor but no growth, a producer gives growth but full operating risk, and the streamer gives **leverage without operating risk**. The mispricing: the cash-flow multiple the market has historically paid WPM was set when it produced ~690k GEO at ~$2,000 gold — but production is **contracted and funded to 1.2M GEO by 2030 (+50%,** management's six new assets *"all funded and permitted,"* no new capital). The *same* multiple on a structurally larger cash-flow base is a higher share price with no re-rating required — which is precisely why WPM is roughly flat-to-up while gold is down 18%. Silver (~49% of revenue, ~1.6× gold beta) is the added torque. The market is extrapolating a depressed spot and under-weighting funded volume growth.

## VALUATION
Our primary tool is a **free-cash-flow-to-equity (FCFE) DCF** with an explicit forecast to 2030 — the end of the funded production ramp — and a terminal value thereafter. It monetizes the thesis directly: revenue is built bottom-up as **volume** (the contracted ramp to 1.2M GEO) × **price** (the sensitivity axis — base gold $4,200 flat, above spot but below street; silver derived via a 1.63 beta). Because per-GEO cost is fixed, margins *expand* as price rises (COGS ~27% of sales in the base, ~22%/34% bull/bear) — the operating-leverage-without-operating-risk mechanism.

On cost of equity, **CAPM gives 10.85% nominal** (Rf 4.95% + β 1.17 × ERP 5.0%), which we show, but we discount the base case at **6% real** for four reasons: (i) the model is in constant dollars, so a real rate is the consistent match; (ii) β 1.17 is almost entirely *gold-price* volatility, which we already model explicitly in the scenario decks — discounting at full β would double-count it; (iii) WPM's residual, price-conditional risk is investment-grade-like (net cash by 2028, no operating or capex exposure); and (iv) **every covering analyst and WPM management discount these streams at 5% — our 6% is already more conservative.** Terminal value blends Gordon growth (g = 1% real) and an 18× EV/EBITDA exit, 50/50.

The base case yields a **$133 target** [EXHIBIT: football-field — bear $90 / base $133 / bull $179 vs $122.60]; the FCFF build independently converges at ~$133, confirming the treatment is internally consistent.

**The multiple is the sanity check, and it points the same way.** Against peers, WPM's ~15.7× EV/EBITDA premium to the ~13.1× median is deserved (scale, growth, margins) and flags no bargain. Against its *own history*, though, WPM's forward P/OCF has compressed to **~17× — the trough multiple it carried in 2016–18 at ~$1,250 gold — versus a ~24× 2019–24 mid-cycle average**, even as cash flow hit a record (~$2.9B) and keeps growing on funded volume. The market is paying a trough multiple on peak, still-growing cash flow: it is **pricing gold mean-reversion into the multiple** — the very bet our floor thesis rejects. If the floor holds, normalization toward mid-cycle on 2027E cash flow implies **~$150–170**, converging with our bull case and the street. We do not bake that re-rate into the base — it is upside optionality, and the compressed multiple is our margin of safety: we are not paying a peak multiple on peak earnings [EXHIBIT: WPM P/OCF vs gold, 2016–26].

## RISKS & SENSITIVITY
The recommendation is, by construction, a view on two variables, and we hold it to **falsifiable tests**:
- **Gold price (the thesis itself)** is our primary sensitivity axis; a sustained return to the $3,400 bear deck takes the target to **$90 (−26%)**. We judge a structural reversal unlikely, but pre-register the break conditions: quarterly central-bank demand holding near the ~290–320t pace (a drop back toward Q1-26's revised 57t would signal the post-2022 regime normalizing), the share of banks funding purchases by selling reserves rising materially above ~38%, and China's Treasury-sale pace. **None has broken** — but the near-term timing risk is real: gold is at YTD lows and positioning is not yet washed out (*right on structure, not yet on timing*).
- **Discount rate / de-rating.** The entire BUY rests on the market continuing to value these cash flows at a streaming/NAV rate. Applying the full **CAPM 10.85% collapses the target to $88** — our honest downside, and roughly where the comparables sit.
- **Silver (~49% of revenue)** is more volatile than gold and its independent industrial-deficit case is contested (BofA models a possible 2026 surplus); we lean on silver's monetary, gold-correlated leg and treat the deficit as optionality.

Diversification cushions idiosyncratic risk — no single mine dominates and ~75% of volume is investment-grade, though holders still bear partner-execution risk (an Antamina disruption, a counterparty default). If gold keeps falling, the asymmetry (−26% bear vs +46% bull) still favors accumulation, and the recommendation stays BUY across the base and bull decks; only a durable return to a real-rate gold regime, or a de-rating to CAPM, flips it.

## CONCLUSION
WPM is the **lowest-risk way to express a gold thesis**: contractual, fixed-cost cash flows that resist record mining-cost inflation; **~50% of fully-funded volume growth that earns a higher valuation even at a lower gold price**; and silver torque for the upside. The market is pricing the financial layer's selloff as if it were structural, while the central-bank bid — now self-reinforced by the fiscal stress its own Treasury selling creates — remains intact. We rate WPM a **BUY to a $133 base target (+8.5%)**, with an asymmetric path to **$179 (+46%)** and limited, quantified downside to $90. **Buy the vehicle, own the thesis.**
