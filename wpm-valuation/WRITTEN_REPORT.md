## Recommendation
We initiate on Wheaton Precious Metals at **BUY**, 12-month target **$145** (FCFE DCF), **+10.5%** above the $131.02 close. The base assumes gold normalizes to $4,500/oz, the central-bank-supported floor that sits above today's ~$3,650 spot and below the street's $4,600 to $5,200 decks. The case is the asymmetry, and the re-rating is already underway: WPM has **outperformed the metal all year (+11% while gold fell -15% and silver -38%) and has firmed further as gold bounced off its November lows**, because the market is pricing a normalized deck and a better business rather than spot. Our bull ($5,200 gold) implies **$187 (+43%)**; our bear ($3,400) implies **$90 (-31%)**. For an investor who wants pure precious-metals exposure with leverage, WPM is the vehicle: it offers the most torque to a gold thesis without a miner's operating risk on the way down.

## Company
WPM is the largest precious-metals streamer: it pays miners an upfront deposit for the right to buy a fixed share of future production at a low, pre-set price (~20% of spot). It operates no mines, so it carries no mine-level operating, cost-inflation or capex risk. Cost is contractually set near $600/GEO, giving **~75% GAAP gross and ~84% EBITDA margins, and a ~86% cash operating margin ex-depletion** (management), with ~47 employees. The book spans **57 assets (22 operating mines) across 19 countries**, ~75% of production from investment-grade counterparties (Vale, Newmont, Glencore) and reserve life beyond 60 years. Production is **contracted and funded to 1.2M GEO by 2030, +50% from 2025**, with management describing the six assets coming online as "all funded and permitted" (the Antamina stream alone is ~18% of 2030 output). No new capital, acquisitions or permitting is required, and the balance sheet reaches net cash by 2028.

## Industry
Royalty and streaming companies finance miners in exchange for a share of production; the peer set is FNV, RGLD and TFPM. Their defining feature is that margins are immune to the cost inflation crushing producers, whose **all-in sustaining costs hit a record $1,930/oz (+22% y/y)** on declining ore grades, deeper mines, and labour and energy inflation. As gold fell ~7% in a recent quarter, miner margins compressed ~16%, downside operating leverage a streamer does not carry. The market pays for the difference: streamers trade at roughly double the producers' EV/EBITDA (**WPM ~19x versus single-digit multiples for large gold producers**), and the gold-heavy royalty leader **FNV trades 20x EV/EBITDA**. FNV is more gold-weighted (~70% gold) and diversified into energy, giving it a lower beta and a defensive profile; WPM is the purer metals play (100% metals, ~49% silver), which gives it the torque, more upside in a gold bull and a deeper, better entry when a washout drags it below fair value.

## Investment thesis
**1. The marginal buyer changed in 2022, so the floor changed.** After the 2022 freeze of ~$300B of Russian reserves re-rated the dollar as a politically contingent asset, central banks replaced price-sensitive Western capital as gold's marginal buyer: price-insensitive, buying on multi-year reserve mandates, clearing the market's excess supply regardless of price. The floor is a *flow*, the pace of that buying (~885t over the trailing four quarters, 321t in Q3-26, +42% y/y), not a line in the spot price.

**2. The selloff is the price-sensitive layer, and the old rule broke.** That layer trades gold against the 10-year real yield, its true opportunity cost; from 2004 to 2021, 100bp of real yield moved gold ~18%. This cycle the real yield rose ~3 points (-1.2% in 2021 to +1.9% at the January-2026 peak, ~2.8% now), a bigger move than 2011's, which the old rule says should have halved gold. It tripled instead. And unlike 2011, when real yields were near zero with room only to rise (a headwind), today they are restrictive with room to fall when the Fed turns (a tailwind), while central-bank buying runs roughly double its 2011 pace.

**3. The bid is self-reinforcing.** About 38% of buying central banks fund purchases by selling other reserves, typically Treasuries; China cut its holdings ~$135B to an 18-year low while adding gold nearly every month. That selling pressures the long end exactly as **US net interest expense has passed defense spending** (the CBO projects ~$16T over the decade, and climbing as yields rise). The 30-year yield sits ~5.3%, near its cycle high, and the Treasury's escalation of long-end buybacks to up to $70B this quarter has not contained it. The diversification creates the fiscal stress that makes diversification more rational, not less.

**4. Lower gold does not mean lower value.** Production is contracted and funded to 1.2M GEO by 2030, so a constant multiple on a larger, growing cash-flow base is a higher share price with no re-rating required, which is why WPM has held up as the metal fell. Silver (~49% of revenue, ~1.6x gold beta, correlation 0.79) adds torque; its independent industrial-deficit case is contested, so we lean on silver's monetary, gold-correlated leg and treat the deficit as optionality. The market is extrapolating a depressed spot and under-weighting the funded growth.

## Valuation
Our primary tool is a **5-year FCFE DCF** (explicit to 2030, then terminal value) discounted at **6% real** cost of equity. We show CAPM (10.85% nominal, 8.15% real; Rf 4.95% + beta 1.18 x ERP 5.0%) and carry it as the downside sensitivity, but discount the base at 6% real on the quality of the cash flows. Conditional on the metal price, which we model separately in the bull/base/bear decks, WPM's cash flows are contractual and investment-grade-like (no operating, cost or capex risk, investment-grade counterparties, net cash by 2028), closer to a corporate bond (~5.5% yield) than to the broad equity market; discounting those price-scenario cash flows at the full equity beta would double-count the gold-price risk the scenarios already carry. So 6% real sits between the IG-credit yield and the 8.15% real CAPM, with the sector's ~5% NAV convention as context, not our basis. The terminal value blends Gordon growth (1% real) and an 18x EV/EBITDA exit, which matches WPM's current trading multiple (18.6x), so it is not a re-rating assumption. Base gold $4,500 yields **$145**; the FCFF build converges.

The multiple corroborates, on two references. Against peers WPM is a deserved premium, not a bargain (peer-median multiples imply ~$103). Against its *own* history it is modestly cheap: its **current-year P/OCF of ~21x (2026E) remains below its ~23x 2019-26 median on the same current-year basis** (and ~18x on next-year cash flow), having compressed with the metal. The clearest evidence is in the tape: **WPM has held near $131 all year while gold fell from ~$4,600 in January to ~$3,650 now, with silver far lower**, so the market is paying the same price for the stock at a much lower metal. That is the re-rating, observable in the price, and it is why we anchor the base to the structural floor ($4,500) rather than spot: holding gold flat at today's ~$3,650 is worth only ~$103 in our model, so at our 6% real rate the market is clearly pricing a deck above spot (equivalently, a higher multiple on current cash flow). Our $4,500 base sits below the street's $4,600 to $5,200 decks, a modest step toward that floor.

## Risks & sensitivity
The call is a view on two variables, held to falsifiable tests. **Gold price** is the primary axis; a sustained $3,400 deck takes the target to $90. We judge an *abrupt* reversal of the central-bank bid unlikely; a *gradual* normalization we would monitor through pre-registered break conditions: quarterly central-bank demand holding near the ~300t pace (Q1's soft 57t was distorted by Turkey's lira-defense selling, not a trend break; Q2 was 296t and Q3 321t), the share of banks funding gold by selling reserves rising above ~38%, and China's Treasury-sale and gold-purchase pace. None has broken. **Discount rate** is the second axis: on the basis-consistent real CAPM (8.15% real) the target is **$117, about -11%**, the main risk if the market demands a full equity premium rather than the ~5% streaming rate (the full nominal CAPM, 10.85%, implies ~$96). **Silver** (~49% of revenue) is more volatile than gold, and it is the torque that overshoots the stock below fair value on washouts, which is also where the entry is. WPM also bears **partner-execution risk**: it does not operate the mines, so its cash flows depend on counterparties producing and delivering, and the **Antamina stream alone is ~18% of 2030 output**, a concentration to watch. The **upside risk**, and our edge, is that the market is already re-rating WPM (it has held ~$131 as the metal fell), so if gold merely stabilizes the re-rating can continue: three independent reads, the DCF bull ($187), WPM's own-history median multiple, and the street (~$185), cluster at ~$170 to $187.

## Conclusion
WPM gives leveraged, pure precious-metals exposure to a gold thesis without a miner's operating risk: fixed-cost cash flows that resist record mining inflation, ~50% of funded volume growth that lifts value even at a lower gold price, and silver torque. The market is pricing a financial-layer selloff as structural while the central-bank bid, now self-reinforced by the fiscal stress its own Treasury selling creates, stays intact, and it has already begun to re-rate a structurally better business. We rate WPM **BUY, target $145 (+10.5%)**, with an asymmetric path to $187.
