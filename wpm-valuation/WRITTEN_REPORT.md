## Recommendation
We initiate on Wheaton Precious Metals at **BUY**, target **$145** (FCFE DCF), **+10.5%** above the $131.02 close. The base assumes gold normalizes to $4,500/oz, the central-bank-supported floor that sits above today's ~$3,650 spot and below the street's $4,600 to $5,200 decks. The case is the asymmetry, and the re-rating is already underway: WPM has **outperformed the metal all year (+11% while gold fell -15% and silver -38%) and has firmed further as gold bounced off its November lows**, because the market is pricing a normalized deck and a better business rather than spot. Our bull ($5,200 gold) implies **$187 (+43%)**; our bear ($3,400) implies **$90 (-31%)**. For an investor who wants pure precious-metals exposure with leverage, WPM is the vehicle: it offers the most torque to a gold thesis without a miner's operating risk on the way down.

## Company
WPM is the largest precious-metals streamer: it pays miners an upfront deposit for the right to buy a fixed share of future production at a low, pre-set price (~20% of spot). It operates no mines, so it carries no mine-level operating, cost-inflation or capex risk. Cost is contractually set near $600/GEO, giving **~75% GAAP gross and ~84% EBITDA margins, and a ~86% cash operating margin ex-depletion** (management), with ~47 employees. The book spans **57 assets (22 operating mines) across 19 countries**, ~75% of production from investment-grade counterparties (Vale, Newmont, Glencore) and reserve life beyond 60 years. Production is **contracted and funded to 1.2M GEO by 2030, +50% from 2025**, with management describing the six assets coming online as "all funded and permitted" (the Antamina stream alone is ~18% of 2030 output). No new capital, acquisitions or permitting is required, and the balance sheet reaches net cash by 2028.

## Industry
Royalty and streaming companies finance miners in exchange for a share of production; the peer set is FNV, RGLD and TFPM. Their defining feature is that margins are immune to the cost inflation crushing producers, whose **all-in sustaining costs hit a record $1,930/oz (+22% y/y)** on declining ore grades, deeper mines, and labour and energy inflation. As gold fell ~7% in a recent quarter, miner margins compressed ~16%, downside operating leverage a streamer does not carry. The market pays for the difference: **Barrick trades ~8.6x earnings and Agnico ~13x, versus WPM ~24x**, and the gold-heavy royalty leader **FNV trades 20x EV/EBITDA**. FNV is more gold-weighted (~70% gold) and diversified into energy, giving it a lower beta and a defensive profile; WPM is the purer metals play (100% metals, ~49% silver), which gives it the torque, more upside in a gold bull and a deeper, better entry when a washout drags it below fair value.

## Investment thesis
**1. The marginal buyer changed in 2022, so the floor changed.** After the 2022 freeze of ~$300B of Russian reserves re-rated the dollar as a politically contingent asset, central banks replaced price-sensitive Western capital as gold's marginal buyer: price-insensitive, buying on multi-year reserve mandates, clearing the market's excess supply regardless of price. The floor is a *flow*, the pace of that buying (~885t over the trailing four quarters, 321t in Q3-26, +42% y/y), not a line in the spot price.

**2. The selloff is the price-sensitive layer, and the old rule broke.** That layer trades gold against the 10-year real yield, its true opportunity cost; from 2004 to 2021, 100bp of real yield moved gold ~18%. This cycle the real yield rose ~3 points (-1.2% in 2021 to +1.9% at the January-2026 peak, ~2.8% now), a bigger move than 2011's, which the old rule says should have halved gold. It tripled instead. And unlike 2011, when real yields were near zero with room only to rise (a headwind), today they are restrictive with room to fall when the Fed turns (a tailwind), while central-bank buying runs roughly double its 2011 pace.

**3. The bid is self-reinforcing.** About 38% of buying central banks fund purchases by selling other reserves, typically Treasuries; China cut its holdings ~$100B to an 18-year low while adding gold nearly every month. That selling pressures the long end exactly as **US net interest expense has passed defense spending** (the CBO projects ~$16T over the decade) and the Treasury has doubled its long-end buyback operations. The diversification creates the fiscal stress that makes diversification more rational, not less.

**4. Lower gold does not mean lower value.** Production is contracted and funded to 1.2M GEO by 2030, so a constant multiple on a larger, growing cash-flow base is a higher share price with no re-rating required, which is why WPM has held up as the metal fell. Silver (~49% of revenue, ~1.6x gold beta, correlation 0.79) adds torque; its independent industrial-deficit case is contested, so we lean on silver's monetary, gold-correlated leg and treat the deficit as optionality. The market is extrapolating a depressed spot and under-weighting the funded growth.

## Valuation
Our primary tool is a 10-year **FCFE DCF** discounted at **6% real** cost of equity. We show CAPM (10.85% nominal; Rf 4.95% + beta 1.18 x ERP 5.0%) but discount the base at 6% because the model is in constant dollars (a real rate is the consistent match), WPM's beta is gold-price volatility that we already model explicitly in the scenario decks (gold's own equity beta is ~0.15), residual risk is investment-grade (net cash by 2028), and the streaming and NAV convention values these cash flows near 5%. Base gold $4,500 yields **$145**; the FCFF build converges at the same number.

The multiple corroborates, on two references. Against peers WPM is a deserved premium, not a bargain (peer-median multiples imply ~$103). Against its *own* history it is cheap: WPM's **2019-26 P/OCF median is ~23x ($168)**, yet it trades ~18x today, at the low end of its range after compressing with the metal. On its 10-year history the multiple has lived in a wide band (washout ~15x to gold-bull peak ~32x), so our scenario targets map to multiples WPM has actually traded at. Finally, WPM is not valued on spot: holding gold flat at today's ~$3,650 is worth only ~$103 in our model, so the market is already pricing a deck above spot, whether through a higher assumed gold path or a higher multiple on current cash flow (the two are observationally the same). Our $4,500 base is a further, modest step toward the structural floor.

## Risks & sensitivity
The call is a view on two variables, held to falsifiable tests. **Gold price** is the primary axis; a sustained $3,400 deck takes the target to $90. We judge an *abrupt* reversal of the central-bank bid unlikely; a *gradual* normalization we would monitor through pre-registered break conditions: quarterly central-bank demand holding near the ~300t pace (Q1's soft 57t was distorted by Turkey's lira-defense selling, not a trend break; Q2 was 296t and Q3 321t), the share of banks funding gold by selling reserves rising above ~38%, and China's Treasury-sale and gold-purchase pace. None has broken. **Discount rate** is the second axis: on the basis-consistent real CAPM (8.15% real) the target is **$117, about -11%**, the main risk if the market demands a full equity premium rather than the ~5% streaming rate (the full nominal CAPM, 10.85%, implies ~$96). **Silver** (~49% of revenue) is more volatile than gold, and it is the torque that overshoots the stock below fair value on washouts, which is also where the entry is.

## Conclusion
WPM gives leveraged, pure precious-metals exposure to a gold thesis without a miner's operating risk: fixed-cost cash flows that resist record mining inflation, ~50% of funded volume growth that lifts value even at a lower gold price, and silver torque. The market is pricing a financial-layer selloff as structural while the central-bank bid, now self-reinforced by the fiscal stress its own Treasury selling creates, stays intact, and it has already begun to re-rate a structurally better business. We rate WPM **BUY, target $145 (+10.5%)**, with an asymmetric path to $187.
